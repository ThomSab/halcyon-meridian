# Labor Unions and Crew Associations Aboard the Halcyon Meridian

## Overview

Workers on the ship are organised into several associations. These are not all unions in the formal sense, because the Articles restrict strikes during voyages (see `economics_and_trade.md`). They still negotiate pay, conditions, and grievances, and they are an important part of the ship's political life.

The associations are recognised under the Articles, and they have the right to:

- Negotiate with the Company on pay, housing allocations, and working conditions
- Represent members in disputes before the Maritime Court
- Nominate candidates for the Residents' Council (see `elections_and_political_parties.md`)
- Run welfare schemes for members

## The Main Associations

| Association | Members | Covers |
|---|---|---|
| **Engineering Mutual** | ~6,000 | Engineers, reactor operators, and maintenance staff |
| **Galley Association** | ~6,500 | Commissary, kitchens, and food production staff |
| **Deck and Navigation Guild** | ~3,500 | Navigation, bridge crew, and deck officers |
| **Sail Crew Association** | ~1,200 | Sail operators and mast technicians |
| **Warden Federation** | ~2,500 | Wardens and security staff |
| **Health Workers' Association** | ~1,800 | Medical and care staff |
| **Freight and Cargo Union** | ~6,500 | Cargo handling, cranes, and freight yard staff |
| **Hotel and Housing Staff Association** | ~11,000 | Housing, cleaning, and terrace services |

Some staff belong to more than one association, and about 15 percent of crew are not members of any. Membership is voluntary.

## Pay Negotiation

Pay is negotiated annually, with a national-style process. The Company sets a budget for pay increases, and each association negotiates within that budget. Where an association cannot agree with the Company, the dispute goes to mediation, which is run by a panel chaired by a Maritime Court judge.

Pay increases are linked to the cost of living on the ship, measured by the Market Authority's price index (see `markets_and_commerce.md`). The index covers the goods that residents buy most often. Associations argue that the index underweights housing, which is a large share of household spending.

## Strikes

Strikes at sea are restricted by the Articles. A work stoppage during a voyage is treated as a serious offence, which can lead to detention and loss of contract (see `governance_and_law.md`). Work stoppages are permitted only:

1. In port, after mediation has failed
2. After a secret ballot of the members, with at least 60 percent in favour
3. With at least 14 days' notice to the Company and the Residents' Council

Stoppages in port are rare. The last major one, in 2070, was a two-day stoppage by the Freight and Cargo Union at a northern European port over safety in the freight yard. It ended after the Company agreed to new brake testing rules for cargo equipment (see `internal_transport.md`).

## Grievances

Individual grievances are first raised with the line manager, then with the department head, and then with the association. If the matter remains unresolved, it can be taken to the Maritime Court. Most grievances are settled at the first or second stage.

## Safety Representatives

Each department elects safety representatives, who have the right to inspect work areas, stop unsafe work, and attend incident investigations. The Engineering Mutual's safety representatives are particularly influential, because reactor and maintenance work is among the most dangerous on the ship (see `reactor_operations_and_nuclear_safety.md` and `maintenance_refits_and_in_water_repair.md`).

## Welfare Schemes

Associations run welfare schemes for members, funded by member subscriptions and matched by the Company. The schemes pay for:

- Funeral costs for members and their families
- Short-term loans for emergencies, through the Meridian Credit Union (see `economics_and_trade.md`)
- Training grants for courses in the Further Education Centre (see `schools_and_education.md`)
- Support after serious injury, in addition to the Company's welfare payments (see `insurance_and_risk_finance.md`)

## Automation Disputes

Automation has been a source of conflict. The Freight and Cargo Union and the Hotel and Housing Staff Association both successfully pressed for a review of automation that affects jobs (see `automation_and_robotics.md`). Under the 2071 agreement, the Company must offer retraining and transfers before any redundancy.

## Relations with the Company

Relations are generally pragmatic. The Company values the associations' role in keeping work safe and in identifying problems early. Some Company managers come from association backgrounds, and senior officers often belong to the Deck and Navigation Guild or the Engineering Mutual. The Board has repeatedly said it wants to avoid the kind of adversarial relations found in some shore industries, because a ship at sea cannot afford a breakdown in trust.

## The Association Hall

Each association has a hall in the Spine's community halls, with a meeting room, a small library, and a training room. Halls are open to members in the evenings and on rest days. The Galley Association hall is the largest, because it is also used for shift-change meetings.
