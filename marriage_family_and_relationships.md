# Marriage, Family, and Relationships Aboard the Halcyon Meridian

## Overview

Family life on the ship is shaped by housing, work rotas, and the ship's population ceiling. Most residents live in family groups of two to four people, and about 38 percent of households are single-person households (see `population_and_demographics.md`). Relationships form and end in the same way they do on shore, but the closed setting and the long voyages add pressures that families must manage.

## Marriage

Marriage is legally recognised under the Articles and the flag state's law. The Master may witness and certify marriages (see `governance_and_law.md`), and chaplains may perform religious ceremonies (see `religion_and_chaplaincy.md`). Couples may marry aboard without any port formalities, but the marriage is recorded in the registry and is recognised by the flag state.

Many couples choose to marry at port calls, because some countries do not recognise ship marriages. The Company's welfare office helps couples arrange a port marriage, and it covers the costs for couples in hardship.

Marriage affects housing. Couples apply for a Grade 1 shared cabin or better, and they must show a stable income or a contract that covers the rent (see `housing_and_residential_terraces.md`). Couples where one partner is a crew member may apply jointly for housing under the crew allocation rules.

## Partnerships

Unmarried couples are recognised for housing, healthcare, and welfare purposes. The Articles treat cohabiting partners on the same basis as married couples for the Company's welfare scheme, provided they have lived together for at least one year or have a child together.

## Divorce and Separation

Separation is handled by the Maritime Court. Divorce is possible under the flag state's law, but the Court may require mediation before granting it. Children's arrangements take precedence, and the Court considers the child's welfare, the family's housing, and any safety concerns.

Separated partners may need to live apart. The Hotel Directorate reallocates housing where this is needed, and the Company's welfare scheme may help with a deposit for a new cabin. Where one partner is a crew member on a fixed contract, the Court may consider the contract's terms.

## Domestic Violence

Domestic violence is treated as a serious offence (see `crime_and_justice_in_practice.md`). The Wardens and the Medical Office have a joint protocol, and the Maritime Court can issue protection orders. Victims can be moved to another cabin or terrace, at the Company's expense, and the perpetrator may be required to leave the cabin.

Support is available from the Medical Office, the Chaplaincy, and a support service run by volunteers from the community halls. The support service operates in the market halls and on the terraces, so that residents can seek help without going to the Warden post.

## Long Voyages

Long voyages are hard on relationships. The most common pressures are:

- Crew working rotating shifts, which make family time difficult
- Crew who are away from their partners for weeks at a time during long contracts
- Loneliness among residents who have family ashore
- Disrupted sleep and mental health problems (see `hospital_and_medical_care.md`)

The Company offers family counselling, and the Chaplaincy runs couples' groups. The Demographic Office's research suggests that couples who have been aboard for more than three years are more likely to report sleep disorders, which can strain relationships.

## Family Leave

Crew and workers may take family leave under the Articles. This includes paternity and maternity leave, carer's leave for elderly relatives, and time off after bereavement. Leave is paid at the usual rate, and the Company guarantees that the worker's job will be held open.

Family leave is especially important when a child is born aboard, because the family's housing and schooling depend on the new arrival being registered (see `population_and_demographics.md`).

## Living Apart

Some families live apart, with one partner on shore and the other aboard. The Company does not require anyone to live aboard, but residents who leave the ship lose their housing and must settle their account. Families who live apart often rely on the intranet and visits at port calls (see `communications_and_intranet.md`).

## Visits and Guests

Partners and family members may visit residents on guest berths (see `immigration_citizenship_and_visitors.md`). A guest berth allows a stay of up to 14 days, and the cost is shared with the resident's housing account.

## Intimacy and Privacy

Cabins are private, and residents are expected to respect each other's privacy. Public displays of affection are normal in the Spine, but overtly sexual behaviour in public is an offence under the Ship's Regulations. Residents may ask the Wardens to intervene if they feel unsafe.

## Support and Advice

The Medical Office runs a family support service with counsellors who can help with relationship problems, separation, and parenting. Referrals can be made through the terrace clinic or the intranet. Advice is confidential, subject to the rules on safeguarding children and protecting people at risk (see `surveillance_privacy_and_data.md`).
