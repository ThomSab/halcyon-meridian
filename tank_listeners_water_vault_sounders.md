# Tank Listeners: The Water Vault Sounders

## The Job

A **tank listener** judges the level of water in the ship's storage tanks by sound. The official job title is Water Vault Inspector, but almost nobody uses it. Listeners work in the double bottom, moving between the forty tanks of the Water Vault (see `water_and_sanitation.md`), and they report what they hear to the Water Department.

The ship has about 30 listeners, all part of the Water and Waste section (see `daily_life_and_routines.md`). They are trained in acoustic methods that the Engineering Department originally developed for checking hull plating, and they use those methods on the tanks.

## What They Listen For

Each tank has a fixed set of sensors, and the Water Department trusts those sensors. The listeners exist because the sensors can fail, and because a sound check can reveal problems that a sensor cannot, such as:

- **Air pockets** in the pipework, which make a hollow ringing when the pumps start
- **Partial blockages** in intakes, which change the pitch of the flow
- **Loose internal fittings**, which knock when the ship rolls
- **Changes in level**, which can be judged by the resonance of the tank wall when tapped

A trained listener can estimate a tank's level to within about 5 percent by tapping the outer wall with a mallet and listening to the note. The method is not exact, and the Water Department uses it only to confirm sensor readings or to raise an alarm.

## The Walk

Listeners work in pairs on a fixed route called **the walk**, which takes about six hours. Each pair visits every tank in the Water Vault, taps the wall, records the note, and checks the sight glass and any visible leaks. The route is changed every quarter, so that listeners do not become complacent about any one tank.

The double bottom is cold, dark, and loud when the pumps run. Listeners wear hearing protection when the RO plant is working, but they take it off to tap and listen. The Company provides thermal suits, and the walk is limited to two hours at a time in the coldest tanks.

## The Sleeping Man

Listeners are the only people who spend regular time among the tanks, and so they are the source of most of the stories about the Sleeping Man (see `ship_legends_and_folklore.md`). Listeners have a private joke about answering knocks from the tanks, and some of them say they do. Their supervisors do not approve, but they do not stop it. The stories help the job attract recruits who are curious about the vault.

## Training

Training takes 18 months. Trainees learn the acoustics of steel and water, the layout of the Water Vault, and the procedures for reporting a suspected fault. They also learn the Water Department's safety rules for confined spaces, which include a buddy system and a rescue harness at every entry point. Trainees must pass a listening test, in which they identify the level of a tank from its note without seeing the sensor reading.

## Relationship with Engineers

Listeners and the Engineering Department have a complicated relationship. Engineers trust sensors and models, and they are sceptical of anything that cannot be measured. Listeners argue that a sensor measures one point, while a tank has a whole body. The best listeners have worked with engineers on sensor placement, and several of the Water Vault's sensors were moved after listeners identified blind spots.

## A Notable Find

In 2070, a listener noticed a change in the note of Tank 17 during a routine walk. The sensor showed a normal reading, but the listener reported a hollow sound near the base of the tank. An inspection found a crack in an internal baffle, which allowed water to slosh and mislead the level sensor. The baffle was repaired during a port call. The incident is still used in training as an example of why both sound and sensors matter.

## Status

Listeners are respected but rarely visible. They are paid at the same rate as other water technicians, and the Water Department has repeatedly asked the Company to create a separate grade for the role. The Board has agreed to review the request in the next pay round (see `labor_unions_and_crew_associations.md`).
