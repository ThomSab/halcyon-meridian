# Emergency Response and Evacuation Aboard the Halcyon Meridian

## The Central Problem

No ship can evacuate 300,000 people at sea. The *Halcyon Meridian* carries survival craft for roughly 25,000 people, about 8 percent of its population. Survival therefore depends on preventing emergencies, containing them on board, and holding the ship together long enough for rescue or repair.

This is why the ship's design focuses on watertight compartments, fire zones, and redundant power, rather than on lifeboats alone.

## Survival Craft

The ship carries:

| Craft | Number | Capacity Each | Total Capacity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Enclosed lifeboats (motor-powered, with fire protection) | 120 | 150 | 18,000 |
| Inflatable life rafts (canopied) | 300 | 25 | 7,500 |
| Rescue boats (fast, for recovering people from the water) | 8 | 20 | 160 |

Survival craft are stored on the upper decks, near the lifeboat stations on each side of the superstructure. They are launched by davits and lowered by winches, which have been tested for load and reliability. The Chief Engineer's department inspects them monthly.

Survival craft are designed for short-term survival. In heavy seas, the lifeboats may be unsafe to launch. The ship relies on rescue vessels to recover people from the water, and on the ship's ability to remain afloat.

## Muster Stations

Every resident is assigned to a muster station, usually on their terrace or in their work area. Muster stations are marked on the decks and listed in each cabin. The Wardens check the attendance of each station against the ship's register, which is updated automatically when people move between zones.

Musters are held monthly, with a drill at a random time once every quarter. Residents are expected to attend, and the Company records attendance. Children and people with disabilities are supported by staff and volunteers at their muster stations.

## Fire Response

The ship is divided into 14 fire zones (see `ship_structure_and_layout.md`). A fire alarm in a zone triggers:

1. Automatic closing of fire doors around the zone
2. Smoke control fans, reversed to extract smoke (see `air_quality_and_ventilation.md`)
3. Water mist activation in the zone
4. A call to the Fire Department, which has about 600 staff on rotating watches

Fire Department crews arrive within three minutes at most locations. The Bridge and the City Operations Centre coordinate the response. Residents in the zone are guided to the nearest fire-free route.

## Flood Response

Each of the 46 watertight compartments has level sensors (see `ship_structure_and_layout.md`). A flood alarm triggers:

1. Closure of watertight doors in the affected compartment and its neighbours
2. Damage control teams sent to the compartment
3. Pumping, using the ship's bilge and ballast systems
4. Ballast adjustment to correct any heel or trim

The ship is designed to survive the flooding of any two adjacent compartments. Damage control teams are trained for more severe cases, including the flooding of three compartments, in which the ship may need to reduce speed and head into the sea.

## Power Loss

If the reactors trip, the diesel generators start automatically. The battery bank (see `propulsion_and_power.md`) supplies critical systems during the transition. The Bridge retains navigation power, and the hospital and fire systems stay powered.

Power loss on a large scale is a serious risk. The ship has been designed so that the emergency generators are high on the superstructure, above the flood level of the lower decks.

## Medical Emergencies

The hospital (see `hospital_and_medical_care.md`) is designed to receive mass casualties. It has a triage system and can expand into the Spine's community halls. The medical emergency plan includes:
- Mass casualty triage in the Spine
- Field clinics on the upper decks
- Coordination with the Chaplaincy for bereaved families

## Storm Subdivision and Storm Law

During heavy weather, the Master may close watertight doors between major zones (Storm Subdivision) and declare Storm Law (see `governance_and_law.md`). These measures reduce the risk of spreading damage but also restrict movement, so they are used only when needed.

## Mass Rescue

The ship's Mass Rescue Plan assumes that a rescue will come from other vessels or from coastal services. The ship has agreements with several coastguard services and maritime rescue coordination centres. In an emergency, the ship broadcasts its position and situation, and the nearest rescue services respond.

Mass rescue at sea is difficult. The number of people involved would far exceed the capacity of any single rescue vessel, so the ship's plan includes staged evacuation to several vessels and the use of shelter aboard the ship itself.

## Drills

The emergency drill schedule is:

| Drill | Frequency |
|---|---|
| Muster | Monthly, plus one unannounced drill per quarter |
| Fire (zone drill) | Monthly |
| Flood damage control | Quarterly |
| Lifeboat launch | Quarterly, with a full launch every year |
| Storm subdivision | Monthly |
| Security (see `piracy_security_and_threats.md`) | Monthly |
| Mass casualty | Twice a year |
| Reactor emergency | Twice a year, with the flag state observing |

Drill results are reported to the Master. Repeated failures in any drill lead to a review by the Board.

## Residents' Role

Residents are required to attend drills and follow the instructions of the Wardens and crew. Residents who are trained in first aid, firefighting, or damage control may volunteer for the Emergency Reserve, which has about 4,000 members and receives extra training and a small stipend.

## Lessons Learned

After the 2072 storm (see `history_and_construction_timeline.md`), the Board ordered a review of the muster process. The review found that the most common delay was residents not knowing the route to their muster station from the terrace. The Company now publishes route maps in every terrace common room and runs a training walk each quarter.
