# Chronists: The Ship's Clock Keepers

## The Job

A **chronist** keeps the ship's time. The title is informal, but the work is official: chronists plan and carry out the one-hour clock adjustments that the ship makes as it crosses longitudes (see `daily_life_and_routines.md`), and they manage the timing of anything that depends on the clock, from shift changes to the opening of the market halls.

The ship has about 20 chronists, based in the Ship Time Office on the Bridge deck and reporting to the Staff Captain. They are part of Administration, and they are the only staff who have a standing right to question the Master's schedule on timing grounds.

## Why the Job Exists

Ship time is a compromise. The clock is set to the departure port and changes by one hour at 03:00 each day as the ship moves east or west. Without someone managing the change, residents would face sudden shifts in sleep, meals, and work at the same time, and the transit fatigue described in the Medical Office's reports would be far worse.

Chronists spread the change across the ship so that nothing happens all at once. The Spine's lights, the rail timetable, the Commissary, the hospital's shift handover, and the water plant's logs each change at different minutes within the window, in a fixed order.

## The Rotation

Each adjustment follows a published sequence called the **rota of the hour**. The rota specifies:

- The minute each system changes, starting with the navigation and power logs and ending with the market halls
- Which systems are held for an extra 15 minutes if a port call or a drill is in progress
- Which residents are notified and how (terrace boards, intranet, and the Spine chimes)

The chronists check each step by hand, and they sign the rota in the paper record kept by the Ship Time Office. This is one of the few record-keeping habits the ship still uses on paper, and the signed rotas are kept in the archive (see `history_and_construction_timeline.md`).

## Disputes

Chronists are often in conflict with other departments. Engineering wants the power logs changed late, after the reactors' overnight checks. The Commissary wants its changes early, before breakfast. The Medical Office wants the hospital to change in the quietest possible minute. Chronists are expected to balance these demands and to write down their reasons when they cannot.

The job carries a particular form of authority. In 2071, a chronist refused to shift a rail timetable during a storm drill, arguing that the change would put trains into a gap in the signals. The Master backed the chronist, and the change was delayed until the drill was over.

## Shore Time

Chronists also answer a common question: what time is it at home? They maintain a reference table of the time difference between the ship and the major ports, and residents may ask for it at the Ship Time Office. The table is also used by residents who keep a home clock (see `shore_clocks_and_home_time.md`).

## Culture and Reputation

Chronists are known on the ship for a dry manner and an obsession with minutes. They often wear a small brass watch on a chain, a habit that started with the first chronists, who were taken from the navigation department. The brass watch is a mark of the trade, and it is given to new chronists when they finish their training.

Children sometimes play a game called "chronist," in which one child calls out the time and the others have to change their actions at the exact minute. The game is common on the Spine and is thought to help children learn to read clocks.

## Training

Chronist training takes 12 months. It covers navigation time, the sequence of ship systems, the Articles' rules on timing, and basic ethics of public information. Trainees shadow a senior chronist through at least two clock changes before they take sole responsibility for one.

## Future of the Role

The Company has considered replacing chronists with automated scheduling. The Board has so far kept the role, because the adjustments involve judgement about people, not just systems, and because the chronists have built trust with residents over the years (see `automation_and_robotics.md`).
