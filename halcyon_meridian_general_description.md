# Halcyon Meridian: General Description

## Overview

The *Halcyon Meridian* is a wind-assisted ocean vessel carrying about 300,000 people across ocean basins on passages lasting two to four weeks. It functions as a floating city: it has housing, farms, hospitals, schools, a court, a police force, a currency, and heavy industry. It runs 24 hours a day, and it is never fully at rest.

## Key Figures

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Length overall | 1,600 m |
| Beam | 320 m |
| Design draft | 42 m |
| Loaded displacement | ~13 million tonnes |
| Hull form | Double-bottom and double-side steel hull, 46 watertight compartments |
| Height, keel to top of bridge | ~150 m above waterline |
| Sail masts | 36 rigid wingsails, ~110 m tall, ~1,500 m² each |
| Nuclear power | 4 small modular reactors (SMRs), 160 MWe each |
| Propulsion | 6 azimuthing electric pod thrusters, 120 MW total |
| Design cruise speed | 9 knots (average ~8 knots including weather) |
| Population | ~300,000 total |
| Crew and operations | ~38,000 |
| Residents | ~262,000, including ~58,000 children under 18 |
| Fresh water production | ~30,000 m³/day by reverse osmosis |
| Freight capacity | ~12,000 TEU on deck and in holds |
| Typical route | Lisbon to Bridgetown, Barbados: ~3,900 nm, ~20 days |

## Plausibility Notes

This is speculative engineering. The figures are internally consistent and drawn from real analogues: large cruise ships and container ships for structure and crew, small modular reactors for power, rigid wingsails for wind propulsion, and commercial reverse osmosis plants for water. The ship as a whole has no precedent. Its displacement is roughly 20 times that of the largest vessel ever built, the *Seawise Giant* (~650,000 tonnes), and its length is about four times that of the largest cruise ships.

The hardest engineering problems are:

- **Hull girder loading.** A 1,600 m hull is longer than many ocean wavelengths in storms. The ship experiences repeated hogging (bow and stern sagging) and sagging (midship sagging) bending moments. Fiber-optic strain sensors along the keel monitor this continuously.
- **Stability.** A 320 m beam and 42 m draft give enough initial stability, but flooding of compartments must be contained. See `ship_structure_and_layout.md`.
- **Turning and stopping.** The ship cannot maneuver quickly. See `navigation_and_weather.md`.
- **Port access.** Few ports have sufficient depth and berth length. Most calls are made at anchor.
