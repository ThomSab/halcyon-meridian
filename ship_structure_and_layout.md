# Ship Structure and Layout of the Halcyon Meridian

## Hull

The hull is built from high-tensile steel (EH36 and EH47 grades). Plate thickness ranges from about 25 mm on the sides to 45 mm at the keel, where stresses are highest. The vessel has a **double bottom** approximately 12 m deep, used for ballast water, fuel, and fresh water storage, and a **double side** about 4 m wide.

The hull is divided into **46 watertight compartments** by transverse and longitudinal bulkheads. Watertight doors are fitted between compartments and can be closed remotely from the Bridge and the City Operations Centre.

### Damage Stability

The ship is designed to survive the flooding of any two adjacent compartments without capsizing. The design target is a static heel of no more than 10 degrees after such damage, with adequate reserve buoyancy remaining. Each compartment has level sensors and flood alarms.

### Hull Girder Loading

A 1,600 m hull is long enough that, in large ocean waves, it may be supported at the crests of two waves at once, or in the troughs. This produces **hogging** (bow and stern drooping) and **sagging** (midsection drooping) bending loads that can be very large.

The hull is designed for worst-case bending moments from ocean waves with significant wave height of 14 m and longer periods. Fiber-optic strain sensors run along the keel and the main deck. They provide real-time readings to the Engineering Department, which adjusts ballast distribution to reduce stress.

## Vertical Layout

From the keel upward, approximately:

| Level | Use |
|---|---|
| Lower double bottom | Ballast, fuel, fresh water reserve (the "Water Vault") |
| Machinery spaces | 4 reactor cells in shielded citadels, pod thruster rooms, switchboards |
| Lower holds | Dry stores, cold storage, spare parts |
| Main cargo deck | Container stacks, freight yard, cargo cranes |
| Deck 5 | Poultry, insect rearing, waste processing |
| Deck 6 to 8 | Aquaculture tanks (RAS), industrial workshops, pharmacy |
| Deck 9 | Spine Run (running track), main Spine level |
| Decks 10 to 22 | Residential terraces (east and west towers) |
| Decks 23 to 28 | Schools, markets, clinics, administration |
| Upper decks | Greenhouses, LED farms, cafés |
| Solarium deck | Translucent-roofed parks, sports fields |
| Superstructure | Bridge, City Operations Centre, sail machinery, bridge deck |

Heights and decks are approximate. The superstructure rises to about 150 m above the waterline, while the wingsail masts extend to about 155 m. The high center of gravity from the superstructure is offset by a deep, heavy double bottom and by anti-roll tanks.

## The Spine

The **Spine** is the central longitudinal boulevard, running about 1.5 km down the centerline of the ship at Deck 9. It is about 40 m wide and runs the full length of the ship's midsection between the two residential towers. It is the city's primary public space.

Along the Spine are:
- Two electric rail lines (about 4 km of track in total), with trains running at up to 25 km/h
- Market halls, cafés, schools, chapels, and community centers
- The Spine Run, a 1.6 km looped track
- Escalators and elevator banks to the terraces above and the holds below

The Spine is lined with transparent panels on its upper edge, allowing daylight from skylights on the Solarium deck to reach the lower levels.

## Residential Towers

Two towers rise from the Spine, forward and aft, containing most of the residential terraces. Each tower is organized into **terraces**: stepped blocks of apartments with shared gardens on their roofs. Terraces are separated by fire barriers every 4 levels. Each terrace has its own elevator core and stairwell.

## Fire Protection

The ship is divided into **14 fire zones**, each sealed by fire doors rated for 60 minutes against fire and smoke. Fire suppression is by water mist, which uses far less water than sprinklers and avoids damage to electronics. Halon is banned under international agreements, and carbon dioxide systems are restricted to machinery spaces.

Ventilation is designed so that smoke can be drawn out of a zone without spreading to adjacent zones. Air handling units recycle about 85 percent of residential air through HEPA filtration and UV treatment.

## Materials

- **Hull and bulkheads:** high-tensile steel
- **Superstructure:** aluminum alloy, chosen to reduce top weight
- **Wingsail masts:** carbon fiber reinforced composite
- **Decks in residential areas:** steel with insulated composite floor panels
- **Greenhouse structures:** aluminum and polycarbonate

## Vertical Transport

About 600 elevators serve the ship. Most are arranged in banks at the terrace cores. Elevator speed is limited to 5 m/s for passenger cars because of the height of the superstructure and the need to maintain stability during motion. Freight elevators are slower and larger.

## Compartment Access During Storms

In heavy weather, the Master may close the watertight doors between major zones, reducing movement between the towers and the holds. This is called **Storm Subdivision**. It is rarely needed in practice, but it is drilled every month.
