# Solid Waste and Recycling Aboard the Halcyon Meridian

## Scale

The ship produces about **270 tonnes of solid waste per day**, roughly 0.9 kg per person. This is in line with a mid-sized city on shore. Liquid waste and blackwater are handled by the water system (see `water_and_sanitation.md`). This file covers everything else.

## Waste Streams

Waste is sorted at source into four streams:

| Stream | Share | Daily Tonnage | Handling |
|---|---|---|---|
| Organic (food and garden waste) | 40% | ~108 t | Composting and insect rearing |
| Recyclables (paper, card, glass, metal, plastic) | 30% | ~81 t | Sorted, baled, and stored for port disposal |
| Residual (non-recyclable general waste) | 30% | ~81 t | Incinerated in heat-recovery boilers |
| Hazardous (batteries, chemicals, medical, electronics) | under 1% | ~2 t | Sealed, stored, and offloaded at port |

Bins are colour-coded on every terrace. The Waste Department employs about 2,200 staff, which includes the water and waste section (see `daily_life_and_routines.md`).

## Organic Waste

Food and garden waste is the largest stream. It is processed in two ways:

- **Composting** in the Waste Processing Halls on Deck 5, where it is turned in aerated windrows. The compost is returned to the greenhouses as fertilizer (see `food_and_agriculture.md`).
- **Insect rearing.** Black soldier fly larvae consume food waste and convert it into protein. The larvae are used as feed for aquaculture tanks and poultry, and some are processed for human food.

Together these routes recover about 90 percent of the organic stream. The remainder is sent to the anaerobic digesters, which produce biogas for the boilers (see `water_and_sanitation.md`).

## Recyclables

Recyclables are sorted by machine and by hand. Paper and card are compressed into bales. Metals are separated magnetically and shredded. Plastics are sorted by type; PET and HDPE are baled, while mixed plastics are sent to the residual stream because they cannot be recycled aboard. Glass is crushed and stored for port recycling.

The ship does not have the capacity to recycle most plastics. Bales are stored in the holds and sold to recyclers at port. The Company is trying to reduce plastic packaging at source, and it bans single-use plastic bags in the market halls.

## Residual Waste

Residual waste is burned in heat-recovery boilers. The heat warms the residential terraces in cold conditions and supplies hot water for laundries and kitchens. Flue gas is cleaned by scrubbers, and ash is stored in sealed bins for port disposal. The incinerator is the largest air emission source on board after the reactors (see `environmental_impact_and_ocean_ecology.md`).

## Hazardous Waste

Hazardous waste is kept separate and labelled. It includes:
- Used batteries, especially lithium-ion (kept in fire-isolated bays)
- Chemicals from clinics, laboratories, and clean rooms
- Medical waste, including sharps and infectious material, sterilized before storage
- Electronic waste

Hazardous waste is stored for up to 180 days before offloading at port. Storage is inspected weekly by the Medical Office and the Fire Department.

## Waste Collection

Waste is collected from terraces by hand carts and small robots, which move along the Spine at night. Collection runs twice daily in the residential towers. Stalls in the markets collect their waste directly into the organic stream.

## Waste Fees

Residents pay a waste service charge, included in the resident fee (see `economics_and_trade.md`). Commercial waste producers pay a volume-based charge, which is intended to encourage separation. Illegal dumping is an offence under the Ship's Regulations, and the Wardens have issued fines for it.

## Dumping at Sea

The Company does not dump waste at sea. This is prohibited under MARPOL, and the Company's environmental permit requires all waste to be landed. The only exception is food waste, which may be discharged beyond 12 nautical miles from land under the MARPOL annex for food waste, although the Company has voluntarily reduced this to zero by using all organic waste for composting and insects.

## Port Offloading

Recyclables, hazardous waste, and residual ash are offloaded at port calls. Offloading is done by lighter at anchorage, or at a berth where one is available. The Waste Department schedules offloading around the port call to avoid delays (see `ports_of_call.md`).
