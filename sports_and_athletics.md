# Sports and Athletics Aboard the Halcyon Meridian

## Why Sport Matters

Physical activity is important on a ship. Residents move less than they would on shore, and the constant motion of the hull is tiring in its own way. Sport gives people exercise, social contact, and something to do during long voyages (see `hospital_and_medical_care.md` for the health picture).

The Company treats recreation as part of resident welfare, and it funds sports facilities from the operating budget. The main constraints are space and water. Pools and sports fields compete with housing and farms for floor area, and any pool uses water that could otherwise go to residents.

## Facilities

| Facility | Location | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Spine Run | Deck 9, the Spine | 1.6 km looped track, open from 05:00 to 23:00 |
| Solarium fields | Solarium deck | Two pitches for football and rugby, one netball court, and a basketball court |
| Gymnasium | Deck 12, aft tower | Weights, cardio machines, and a climbing wall |
| Indoor pool | Deck 7 | 25 m, using treated greywater and heated by waste heat (see `water_and_sanitation.md`) |
| Squash and racquet courts | Deck 12 | Four courts, booked by slot |
| Table tennis and martial arts rooms | Community halls | Shared with clubs |
| Cycle lanes | Outer Spine | Electric bikes only, speed limit 15 km/h (see `internal_transport.md`) |

The pool is the most water-intensive sports facility. It holds about 500 cubic metres, and it is topped up from the greywater system. The pool is closed for one day a month for cleaning and water testing, and the Medical Office samples it weekly.

## Solarium Pitches

The Solarium fields are under translucent roof panels, which let in daylight but keep off the worst of the weather. In storms, the panels are closed and the fields are used for indoor training. Players are used to the ship's motion, and the pitches are designed with a slight slope to reduce the effect of rolling.

Football is the most popular sport, with leagues organised by terrace and by department. Rugby is strong among crew, particularly the Deck and Navigation Guild. Netball has a large female following and a league of its own.

## Clubs and Leagues

About 80 sports clubs are registered with the Company's Recreation Office. Clubs must have at least 12 members, a named coach, and a safety officer. Leagues run on a seasonal basis, with about 40 weeks of play in a typical year.

Major events include:

- **The Landfall Cup**, a football tournament held after each major port call (see `arts_media_and_entertainment.md` for the Landfall Festival)
- **The Halcyon Relay**, a 1.6 km team relay on the Spine Run, held on Departure Day (see `calendar_holidays_and_festivals.md`)
- **Crew Games**, an annual multi-sport event between departments, with a trophy held by the Sail Department as of the most recent year

## Coaching and Training

Coaches must hold a recognised qualification from their home country or pass the Recreation Office's coaching course. Coaches are paid a small stipend from the Recreation budget. Children's coaches must also hold a safeguarding certificate (see `childhood_and_growing_up_aboard.md`).

## Sport and Motion

Playing sport on a moving ship has unusual challenges. Balance is affected by roll, and balls can travel unpredictably. The Recreation Office publishes safety guidance for each sport, and it reduces play when the roll exceeds 6 degrees. Storms close all outdoor and Solarium sports areas.

## Health Benefits

The Medical Office's studies show that residents who take part in regular sport report better sleep, lower stress, and fewer falls among older residents. The Company promotes exercise through the Health Week each year, when clinics offer free fitness checks and the Spine Run hosts a community walk.

## Costs

Sports facilities are free to use for residents, subject to slot bookings. Some clubs charge a membership fee, which covers equipment and travel within the ship. Equipment is provided by the Company for most sports, but residents may buy their own through the Harbour Hall.

## Competitions Between Ships

The ship has never hosted a competition with another vessel, because the Company has no regular contact with other ships at sea. Some residents have proposed matches during joint port calls, and the Recreation Office is considering a pilot with another ship on a shared anchorage in the Caribbean.

## Accidents

Most sports injuries are sprains and minor fractures. Falls on the Spine Run and collisions on the Solarium pitches are the most common causes. The Medical Office keeps a register of sports injuries, which is used to improve the surfaces and rules. Serious injuries are reported to the Recreation Office and reviewed each year.
