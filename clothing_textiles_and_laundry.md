# Clothing, Textiles, and Laundry Aboard the Halcyon Meridian

## Why Clothing Is a Problem at Sea

Clothing is one of the hardest supplies to manage on a closed ship. Fabric takes up storage space, it needs water and energy to wash, and it wears out faster in a humid, salty environment. The ship's population of 300,000 generates a large and constant demand for laundry, while the holds have limited room for spare clothing.

The Company's approach is to keep clothing simple, durable, and easy to clean, and to recycle textiles where possible.

## Supply

Clothing comes into the ship through three routes:

1. **Port purchases**, bought at anchorage markets or through the Harbour Hall (see `markets_and_commerce.md`)
2. **Company supply**, including uniforms and work clothing issued to crew and staff
3. **Personal stock**, brought aboard by residents at joining, subject to a storage limit of 0.5 m³ per adult (see `housing_and_residential_terraces.md`)

Textile imports are a significant freight item. Clothing is one of the most common consumer goods in the Harbour Hall, and it is price-controlled only for basic items.

## Uniforms

Crew wear uniforms that are colour-coded by department (see `language_culture_and_social_classes.md`):

| Department | Colour |
|---|---|
| Officers | Navy with rank insignia on the shoulders |
| Engineering and reactors | Grey |
| Deck and navigation | Dark green |
| Medical | White |
| Galley and food production | White with a blue band |
| Wardens | Dark blue with a reflective band |
| Sail Department | Orange, for visibility on the masts |
| Cargo and freight | Hi-vis yellow with grey trousers |

Uniforms are issued on joining, with two sets for daily wear and one set for formal occasions. They are replaced every 18 months, or sooner if damaged. Crew who leave the ship return their uniforms to the Stores, where they are cleaned, repaired, or recycled.

## Laundry

Laundry is provided in every terrace, and in the crew blocks. There are about 180 laundry rooms in the residential towers, each with 12 to 20 machines. The machines are industrial-grade and designed for water efficiency.

Each adult receives a free laundry allowance of about 8 kg of washing per week, which is included in the free water allowance (see `water_and_sanitation.md`). Extra washing is charged at a fixed rate in ledger credits.

Washing uses recycled greywater for the first rinse and treated water for the final rinse. Hot water is supplied from the hot loop (see `water_and_sanitation.md`), and the temperature is set to 60 degrees Celsius for sanitary washing and lower for delicate items.

## Drying

Drying indoors is a problem because of humidity (see `air_quality_and_ventilation.md`). Most laundry is dried in heated drying rooms with dehumidifiers, and the exhaust air is recovered to reduce energy use. Outdoor drying is possible on the Solarium deck, but it is limited by weather and by the need to keep the decks clear for maintenance and safety.

Mould is a risk in poorly ventilated drying rooms, and the Hotel Directorate checks them each week.

## Textile Recycling

Worn textiles are sorted into three groups:

- **Wearable**, cleaned and passed on through the Hand-me-Down Exchange in the Central Hall
- **Repairable**, sent to the tailoring workshops for mending and alteration
- **Non-wearable**, cut into wiping cloths for workshops, or sent to the residual waste stream if they are too contaminated (see `solid_waste_and_recycling.md`)

Textiles are a large share of the ship's recyclables, but synthetic fabrics cannot be recycled aboard and are stored for port disposal.

## Tailoring

The ship has about 60 tailors and seamstresses, based in the Harbour Hall and in the terrace community rooms. They repair clothing, alter uniforms, and make simple garments from fabric bought in port. Tailoring is one of the most common small businesses on the market halls (see `markets_and_commerce.md`), and it is licensed by the Market Authority.

## Shoes and Protective Gear

Non-slip shoes are required in working areas, and the Company provides them to crew. Residents must buy their own non-slip shoes for work in those areas. The Medical Office recommends that residents with mobility problems have shoes with good grip, because falls are common in rolling seas (see `elder_care_and_end_of_life.md`).

Protective gear, including gloves, harnesses, and high-visibility vests, is issued to workers who need it. The Sail Department and the Maintenance Department hold their own stores of protective equipment.

## Fashion

Residents dress in a wide range of styles, drawn from home countries and from the Harbour Hall's imports. Fashion on the ship is shaped by practicality. Lightweight, quick-drying fabrics are popular, and heavy coats are rare except for crew on the upper decks in cold weather. The Spine's shops sell a mix of local and imported clothing.

## Cold Weather

The ship's route passes through cold conditions on the North Atlantic leg, and at high latitudes in winter. Residents on the upper decks may need warm clothing, which is sold in the Harbour Hall. Crew on the Solarium and the masts receive insulated outerwear from the Company.

## Textile Standards

Textiles sold on the ship must meet the Market Authority's standards for flammability, colourfastness, and chemical safety. Children's clothing must meet additional standards, and the Medical Office checks a sample of each shipment.
