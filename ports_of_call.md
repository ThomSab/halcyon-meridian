# Ports of Call for the Halcyon Meridian

## Why Ports Are a Problem

The ship's size makes port calls difficult. It needs water at least 45 m deep and a berth more than 1.7 km long. The Company's 2063 survey found very few ports that met both requirements, and most of those are constrained by channels, bridges, or port authorities who do not want a vessel of this size.

For this reason, most calls are made at **anchorage**. The ship drops anchor offshore, and freight and people are transferred by lighters and tenders (see `economics_and_trade.md`). A typical call takes three to five days.

## Port Call Pattern

The ship calls at a port every **45 to 90 days**, depending on fuel, food stock, and freight contracts. Calls are planned at the start of each voyage, and the Chief Operating Officer is responsible for securing port agreements.

A typical call includes:

1. **Arrival and anchoring.** A pilot boards, and the ship anchors in the planned position.
2. **Immigration and customs.** Port officials board by tender. Residents leaving or joining are processed.
3. **Freight transfer.** Containers and bulk cargo are lightered in and out.
4. **Stores.** Dry stores, fresh food, and spare parts are loaded.
5. **Offloading.** Recyclables, hazardous waste, and residual ash are removed (see `solid_waste_and_recycling.md`).
6. **Crew and resident movement.** Residents may go ashore by tender, subject to port rules and the Articles.
7. **Departure.** The ship weighs anchor and resumes its route.

## Typical Ports

The ship's regular calls are in three areas:

- **Atlantic Europe**, including the Lisbon anchorage off Cascais, which is the usual departure point for the Atlantic route
- **The Caribbean**, including the Bridgetown anchorage, where the ship usually anchors offshore because the harbour is too shallow for its draft
- **Northwest Africa and the Gulf of Guinea**, used for freight contracts, but avoided where possible because of piracy risk (see `piracy_security_and_threats.md`)

Other calls are made in response to freight demand and fuel and food supply. Since the ship's reactors need refuelling every five to seven years, one of the ports on the schedule must have licensed nuclear handling facilities.

## Port Costs

Port costs include anchorage fees, pilotage, tug and lighter charges, customs fees, and port-state inspection costs. Port costs are about 12 percent of the ship's operating budget, and they vary widely by location.

## Port Access for Residents

Residents may go ashore during port calls if they are not under restrictions and have paid any outstanding ledger balances. Tenders run on a fixed schedule, typically every two hours during daylight. Residents who cannot afford the trip may remain aboard, and the Company does not charge for staying.

## Resident Departures

At the end of a contract, a resident may leave at any port call. The Articles allow the Company to require a notice period of 30 days for workers in critical roles, such as engineers and medical staff. Departure is recorded by the Master, and the resident's share of any ledger balance is settled at the port exchange office.

## Port Authorities

Each port has its own authorities, and the ship must comply with their rules. These usually include:

- Pilotage requirements
- Port state control inspections under international conventions
- Environmental rules on discharge, emissions, and waste
- Health checks, which may include quarantine screening

The Company's legal team maintains a register of port requirements, updated before every call.

## Weather and Port Delays

Anchorage is vulnerable to weather. In heavy swell or wind, lighter operations may stop for days, and the ship may be forced to leave anchorage early. The Chief Operating Officer keeps a contingency budget for delays, and the Master has the final say on whether conditions are safe.

## Local Relations

The Company sponsors a small community programme in each regular port, including school visits, donations of food and medical supplies, and cultural exchanges. The programme is intended to maintain good relations, which are important for future port access. The programme budget is about 0.5 percent of revenue.
