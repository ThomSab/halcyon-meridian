# Housing and Residential Terraces Aboard the Halcyon Meridian

## The Terraces

Most residents live in the **residential towers**, which rise from the Spine on decks 10 to 22. Each tower is divided into **terraces**, stepped blocks of apartments. Each terrace is four levels high, with apartments opening onto a shared garden terrace on the roof of the level below. Fire barriers separate terraces, and each has its own elevator core and stairwell.

The east and west towers together hold about 230,000 residents. The remaining residents live in the crew blocks and in smaller housing blocks forward of the Spine.

## Living Space

Average living space is about **15 m² per person**. This is the most scarce resource on the ship. Housing is allocated by grade:

| Grade | Type | Space | Typical Occupants | Rent (LC/month) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Grade 3 | Bunk pod, shared | ~6 m² per person | Single workers, contract staff | ~350 |
| Grade 2 | Single cabin | 9 to 12 m² | Single adults | ~600 |
| Grade 1 | Shared two-room cabin | 25 to 30 m² | Couples, pairs, small families | ~900 |
| Grade 0 | Family suite | 45 to 60 m² | Families with children | ~1,500 |

Rents are set by the Hotel Directorate (see `economics_and_trade.md`). Grade 0 suites are scarce and are allocated mainly to families of crew and senior staff.

## Cabin Features

All cabins have:
- A sway-compensated bunk or bed
- A vacuum toilet and a small shower (see `water_and_sanitation.md`)
- Individual climate control
- A fold-down desk and a wall screen linked to the intranet
- Lockable storage, with a size limit of 0.5 m³ per adult

Grade 0 suites add a kitchenette with a small refrigerator, a second bathroom, and a balcony overlooking the Spine. Balconies are on the outer edge of the towers and are limited to one per family.

## Noise and Vibration

Cabins on the lower terraces are closer to the pod thrusters and feel more vibration. The Hotel Directorate sets a target sound level of 40 decibels in bedrooms at night. Noise from the Spine is strongest at the lower terraces, and bedroom windows are double-glazed on the Spine side.

## Allocation

Housing is allocated by the Hotel Directorate on the basis of employment, family size, and length of service. New arrivals usually start in Grade 3 or Grade 2 and may move up after a year without a serious conduct record. Waiting lists are long for Grade 1 and Grade 0.

## Maintenance

Maintenance crews carry out routine repairs in cabins. Residents report faults through their terminal or the terrace clinic. The Hotel Directorate aims to complete routine repairs within 72 hours and emergency repairs within two hours.

## Inspections

Each terrace is inspected every three months for fire safety, water quality, mould, and structural condition. Mould is treated as a priority issue, because the humid air of the towers can support it in unventilated corners.

## Social Life on the Terraces

The shared garden terraces have seating, planters, and a small children's play area. Each terrace has a common room, a laundry, and a shared kitchen for residents who do not have one. Terrace committees elect a representative to the Residents' Council (see `governance_and_law.md`).

## Housing and Income

Housing consumes about 35 to 40 percent of a typical wage, as noted in `economics_and_trade.md`. Residents whose housing costs exceed 45 percent of income may apply for a rent reduction through the Hotel Directorate's hardship scheme.

## Elder Housing

Older residents may apply for the **Harbour Terraces**, a set of accessible apartments on the lower terrace levels, with step-free access, grab rails, and proximity to the care complex (see `elder_care_and_end_of_life.md`).
