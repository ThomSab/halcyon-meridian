# Environmental Impact and Ocean Ecology Around the Halcyon Meridian

## Overview

A ship of this size affects the ocean in many ways, even though its main energy source is wind and nuclear power. This file lists the main impacts, the controls the Company uses, and the open problems it still faces. The Company is subject to the IMO's MARPOL convention, the flag state's environmental rules, and port state requirements.

## Emissions to Air

| Source | Emissions | Controls |
|---|---|---|
| Reactors | No direct combustion emissions | Sealed primary loop; heat rejected to seawater |
| Diesel generators | NOx, SOx, particulate matter, CO2 | Used only as backup; fuel is low-sulfur; exhaust scrubbers |
| Incinerators (residual waste) | CO2, particulate matter, acid gases | Scrubbers, filters, and heat recovery |
| Biogas boilers | CO2, small amounts of NOx | Used in place of fossil fuel where possible |
| Galley and terrace kitchens | Fine particulate matter | Ducted extraction with filters |

The ship's total emissions are much lower than those of a similar-sized cargo ship that burns heavy fuel oil. Most of its propulsion comes from wind and nuclear power, and its carbon emissions are dominated by diesel backup use and incineration.

## Discharges to Water

The main discharges are:

- **Brine** from the reverse osmosis plant, at about 35 m depth, behind the ship (see `water_and_sanitation.md`)
- **Treated greywater and blackwater**, which are treated before discharge and mostly reused (see `water_and_sanitation.md`)
- **Cooling water** from the reactors and machinery. This is seawater taken in at one temperature, warmed by a few degrees, and returned to the ocean. Discharge temperature is monitored.
- **Bilge and oily water**, treated by separators and monitored by oil content meters
- **Food waste**, discharged only when comminuted and at a distance from land permitted by MARPOL, although the Company has largely stopped doing so (see `solid_waste_and_recycling.md`)

The Company monitors the water around the ship for temperature, salinity, oxygen, and oil. Results are published annually in the environmental report.

## Ballast Water

Ballast water is taken on and released to balance the ship. It can carry organisms from one ocean to another, which may become invasive. The ship's ballast system uses UV treatment, which kills most organisms, and the Company records each ballast exchange in the ship's logbook. The ship avoids ballast exchange in coastal waters where possible.

## Antifouling

Marine organisms can grow on the hull and reduce speed. The hull uses a silicone-based, biocide-free antifouling coating, which allows organisms to be released by hydrodynamic action at speed. At low speed or at anchor, the hull can accumulate growth, which is removed by divers and underwater robots during maintenance (see `economics_and_trade.md`).

The Company avoids copper-based paints in the wake of the ship, because of their effect on marine life in the ship's wake.

## Underwater Noise

Ship propulsion, pods, and machinery produce underwater noise, which can disturb whales and other marine mammals. The pods are designed to produce less noise than conventional propellers, and the Company reports noise levels to the flag state. The ship slows near known marine mammal areas when it can, and the Navigation Officer checks for sightings on the route.

## Collisions with Marine Life

Large ships can strike whales and other animals. The Lookout Department and the Bridge team watch for animals on the route, and the Navigation Officer reports sightings to the regional marine research network. In the first ten years of operation, there were four confirmed collisions, all with animals that could not be seen in time. The Company has since added thermal cameras on the Bridge to improve detection at night.

## Light Pollution

External lights can disturb seabirds and marine life. The Company dims external deck lights at night and uses shielded fittings where possible (see `daylight_and_lighting.md`). The masthead and navigation lights are required by law and cannot be dimmed.

## Waste and Litter

The ship does not discharge garbage at sea. All solid waste is landed or processed on board (see `solid_waste_and_recycling.md`). Single-use plastics are banned in the markets, and the Company's packaging rules apply to imported goods.

## Radioactive Materials

The reactors produce spent fuel, which is stored in a pool in the reactor citadels for several years to allow it to cool. When the reactors are refuelled, spent fuel is removed by a licensed carrier at a port with the right facilities (see `ports_of_call.md`). The Company does not store spent fuel in the ocean or aboard the ship beyond the cooling period.

Low-level radioactive waste from maintenance and the hospital is stored in sealed containers and removed at refuelling. The Chief Engineer holds the nuclear licence and reports to the flag state.

## Greenhouse and Agricultural Impacts

Aquaculture and greenhouses use water and nutrients. The Company recycles about 95 percent of nutrient solution (see `food_and_agriculture.md`), and it avoids using antibiotics in fish tanks. Fish waste is used as fertiliser, not discharged.

## Carbon Accounting

The Company publishes an annual carbon account. The 2073 account showed that the ship's carbon intensity per passenger-kilometre was lower than that of a large cruise ship and a comparable container ship, mainly because of wind propulsion and nuclear power. The Company has committed to offsetting the remaining emissions from diesel backup and incineration.

## Open Problems

The Company's own list of unresolved environmental problems includes:
- Heat and salinity from brine and cooling water, which may affect coastal ecosystems in enclosed waters
- Ballast water in regions where UV treatment is less effective
- Underwater noise, which is hard to reduce further without changing the propulsion design
- The long-term fate of spent nuclear fuel, which depends on shore facilities the Company does not control
- The limited ability to recycle plastics aboard (see `solid_waste_and_recycling.md`)

## Fishing

The Company does not allow commercial fishing from the ship. Fish stocks near the route are managed by national authorities, and the Company's aquaculture is designed to reduce dependence on wild fish (see `food_and_agriculture.md`). Residents may fish recreationally from designated areas on the Solarium deck, but catch must be recorded, and fish below the minimum size must be released.
