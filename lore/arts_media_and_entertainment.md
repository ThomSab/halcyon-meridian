# Arts, Media, and Entertainment Aboard the Halcyon Meridian

## Constraints

Entertainment on board is shaped by three constraints:

- **Bandwidth.** The ship's satellite link carries about 8 Gbps in total (see `daily_life_and_routines.md`). Streaming a film to thousands of screens at once is impossible, so almost all media is stored and distributed on the local network.
- **Space.** Venues compete with housing and farms for floor area, so the Company licenses only a limited number of them.
- **Cost.** Entertainment is paid for in ledger credits, and most residents have modest budgets.

## Library

The **Meridian Library** holds about 500,000 items, including books, periodicals, maps, music, films, and games. It occupies two decks on the Spine's upper concourse. The collection is updated at each port call, when new material is loaded from port libraries and bookshops. Books are lent for 21 days, and loans are free.

The library also runs a maritime archive with logbooks, navigation charts, and recordings from earlier voyages. Researchers may request access through the Archive Office.

## Cinema

The Deck 24 cinema has four screens and about 900 seats in total. Films are stored on local servers and screened on a fixed schedule. Tickets cost 6 to 12 LC, depending on time and seat. The most popular films are those from the port library updates, and the cinema holds a monthly screening of films made aboard.

## Theatre and Music

The **Spine Theatre**, on the Spine's central concourse, seats about 1,200 people and hosts plays, concerts, and dance. Performers are drawn from residents, and the Company pays a modest fee for each performance. Rehearsal space is in the community halls.

Music is common. Choirs and bands are among the most popular clubs. The Company licenses live music in bars, and it allows recorded music in the terraces, subject to noise limits (see `housing_and_residential_terraces.md`).

## Visual Arts

Artists work in the **Studio Terraces** on decks 20 and 21, which provide cheap studio space and natural light from the terrace gardens. Work is sold at the Harbour Hall and in a gallery on the Spine. The Company takes a 15 percent commission on sales.

Paint and materials are restricted, because some solvents are classed as hazardous and must be stored in sealed containers (see `solid_waste_and_recycling.md`). Artists are encouraged to use water-based materials.

## Media

Residents receive the **Halcyon Gazette**, a daily news bulletin on the intranet, produced by a team of about 40 journalists. The Gazette covers ship affairs, port news, and selected world news from the satellite feed. It is funded by the Company, and its editorial policy is set by a board that includes one Residents' Council representative. The Gazette has been criticized for covering the Company's decisions too favourably, and the Council has asked for a formal independence review.

There is no broadcast television. Residents watch recorded programmes from the library or from a catalogue of licensed shows updated at port calls.

## Games and Leisure

Board games, card games, and video games are popular. The Company permits gaming in the community halls, with a licence fee for gaming venues. Gambling is licensed only for small-stake games, with a cap of 20 LC per bet, and is monitored by the Wardens.

## Festivals and Events

Performances, exhibitions, and screenings are scheduled around the ship's calendar (see `calendar_holidays_and_festivals.md`). The biggest annual event is the **Landfall Festival**, held on the Spine after each major port call.

## Censorship

The Articles of Agreement guarantee freedom of expression. The Company may, however, restrict material that threatens safety, incites violence, or breaches the flag state's laws. Decisions on restrictions are made by the Market Authority for public venues and by the Maritime Court for disputes.
