# History and Construction of the Halcyon Meridian

## Origins

The project began with a feasibility study commissioned by Meridian Maritime Holdings in 2054. Earlier proposals for large floating cities had failed on cost, stability, and legal grounds. The study concluded that a vessel carrying 300,000 people was technically possible if it combined a very large steel hull, rigid wingsails, and small modular reactors, and if its freight business could subsidize the cost of housing its residents.

The name *Halcyon* comes from the mythical seabird said to calm the waves. Investors chose it for its marketing value. Residents sometimes call the ship "the Bird" or simply "the Meridian."

## Why the Population Grew Slowly

The ship did not sail full. Housing, food, water, and medical capacity were commissioned in stages so that each block could be stocked and staffed before residents moved in. The first cohort lived in roughly a third of the terraces. Full capacity took three years and was delayed twice by problems with the greenhouse lighting and the water intake screens.

## Construction Problems

Several problems from the build shaped later practice:

- **Hull welding.** Some early welds in the keel sections failed ultrasonic inspection. About 2 percent of the keel weld length was re-done in the basin, adding eleven months to the schedule.
- **Reactor cell shielding.** Concrete placement in the lower citadels had to be repeated after thermal cracking. Shielding specifications were revised for later blocks.
- **Mast vibration.** The first wingsail prototype showed resonance at a frequency close to the pod thrusters. The fix was a change to the mast stiffness and damping, which is why the masts are fitted with tuned mass dampers at their bases.

## Ship Records

The original build records, including the weld logs and reactor cell certifications, are kept in the Engineering archive on Deck 4. A copy is held at the flag registry. These records are opened to the Maritime Court in any hull or reactor dispute.

## The Ship's Anniversary

The ship's anniversary is celebrated on the date of the maiden departure. See `calendar_holidays_and_festivals.md`.
