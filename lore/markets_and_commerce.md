# Markets and Commerce Aboard the Halcyon Meridian

## Overview

The ship's retail economy runs through four covered **market halls** along the Spine, plus about 900 stalls and shops in the residential terraces. Together they sell food, clothing, electronics, household goods, and services. Prices are in ledger credits (LC), as described in `economics_and_trade.md`.

Commerce on board is mostly small-scale and regulated. The Company sets the rules for what can be sold, by whom, and at what price for essentials.

## The Four Halls

| Hall | Location | Specialty |
|---|---|---|
| Forward Hall | Deck 9, forward end of the Spine | Fresh produce and greenhouse goods |
| Central Hall | Deck 9, midpoint | General goods, clothing, household items |
| Aft Hall | Deck 9, aft end | Fish, poultry, and prepared food |
| Harbour Hall | Deck 8, near the freight lifts | Imported goods from port calls, electronics, tools |

Each hall has a licensing office run by the Market Authority, a Company body that manages trading licences, weights and measures, and complaints.

## Who Sells What

Most stalls are run by residents on licence. A licence costs 150 LC per month and requires passing a food-hygiene course for food stalls. Licences are not transferable. Larger businesses, such as the Central Hall's clothing shops, are run by Company subsidiaries.

Shops in the terraces are mostly small convenience counters selling dried goods, toiletries, and simple snacks.

## Price Controls

Essentials are price-controlled:

| Item | Controlled Price (LC) |
|---|---|
| 1 kg rice | 6 |
| 1 kg dried lentils | 5 |
| 1 kg greenhouse tomatoes | 18 |
| 1 kg tilapia | 14 |
| 1 litre cooking oil | 9 |
| 1 bar of soap | 2 |

The Market Authority reviews these prices every quarter. Price changes above 5 percent require approval from the Residents' Council, and the Council can call a referendum on large increases (see `governance_and_law.md`).

Non-essential goods are not controlled. Electronics, clothing, and alcohol are priced by supply and demand, within the limits set by the Market Authority for fair trading.

## Imports

Goods come aboard at port calls (see `ports_of_call.md`). Freight is loaded in the cargo holds and transferred to the Harbour Hall. Freight rates and the costs of lighterage (see `economics_and_trade.md`) make imported goods expensive. A bag of coffee that costs a few dollars at port may cost the equivalent of several hours of a skilled worker's wage aboard.

Because the ship spends most of its time at sea, supplies run down between port calls. The Market Authority sets a stocking target for each hall, and stalls can be empty in the last two weeks before a port call. Staples are never allowed to run out, because the Commissary has priority access to them.

## Cash, Credit, and Barter

Most payments are made through ledger credit cards linked to resident accounts. Cash exists only as small Company tokens, used for vending machines and small stalls. Some informal barter takes place for services, such as haircuts in exchange for laundry, but this is not recognized by the Company and is not taxed.

## Advertising

The Market Authority restricts advertising to notice boards in the halls. Television and screen advertising is banned in the terraces, because of the limited bandwidth and the Company's wish to reduce spending pressure on residents.

## Consumer Protection

Stalls must display prices and weights. Food sold must carry a freshness date and an origin label. Complaints are heard by the Market Authority, and repeated breaches lead to licence suspension. The Market Authority also investigates the shadow trade (see `economics_and_trade.md`).

## Opening Hours

Halls open at 07:00 and close at 22:00 ship time. Food stalls in the Aft Hall open until 01:00 for night shift workers.
