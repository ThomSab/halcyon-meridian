# Air Quality and Ventilation Aboard the Halcyon Meridian

## Why It Matters

Three hundred thousand people breathing the same enclosed air is a significant engineering problem. The air on board must be clean, cool, dry enough to avoid mould, and free from dangerous gases. Poor air quality spreads respiratory infections, causes headaches and fatigue, and, in a fire, can kill faster than heat.

## Ventilation System

The ship's air is moved by about **4.5 million cubic metres per hour** of ventilation capacity, provided by roughly 1,400 air handling units. These units are distributed across the decks, with large intake and exhaust shafts running the height of the superstructure.

The system works in three main loops:

1. **Residential loop.** About 85 percent of air is recirculated. Recirculated air passes through HEPA filters and UV treatment before returning to the rooms. Fresh outside air makes up the rest, drawn from high intakes on the superstructure, away from exhaust and funnel emissions.
2. **Commercial and industrial loop.** Halls, markets, and workshops use mixed recirculation with local exhaust for fumes, dust, and cooking vapour.
3. **Isolation loop.** The Quarantine Ward and other isolation spaces are kept at negative pressure. Air is exhausted through HEPA and UV filters and never recirculated (see `hospital_and_medical_care.md`).

## Targets

The Hotel Directorate sets the following targets for residential areas:

| Parameter | Target |
|---|---|
| Carbon dioxide | Below 1,000 ppm on average |
| Temperature | 20 to 24 degrees Celsius |
| Relative humidity | 40 to 60 percent |
| Fresh air supply | Minimum 20 m³/hour per adult |
| Particulate matter (PM2.5) | Below 15 µg/m³ on average |

Carbon dioxide is the main constraint in crowded areas. The Spine's market halls and the rail platforms can exceed 1,000 ppm at peak times, and the ventilation system increases fresh air supply automatically when sensors show a rising level.

## Sensors

The ship has about **40,000 air sensors**. They measure carbon dioxide, humidity, temperature, particulate matter, and volatile organic compounds. Readings are sent to the City Operations Centre every five minutes. Alarms trigger automatically when a reading goes outside its target range.

## Humidity and Mould

Humidity is the main long-term problem. The terraces are humid, and mould can appear in corners, behind furniture, and around cold surfaces. The Hotel Directorate treats mould as a priority repair and checks the most exposed units each month (see `housing_and_residential_terraces.md`). Dehumidifiers run continuously in laundry rooms and in the lower terraces.

## Fire Smoke Control

Smoke control is a separate system. In a fire zone, the exhaust fans reverse direction to draw smoke out of the zone and keep the neighbouring zones at positive pressure. Fire doors seal each zone, and the system is tested every month during muster drills (see `emergency_response_and_evacuation.md`).

## Odours and Cooking

Cooking exhaust in the Commissary and the terrace kitchens is ducted separately. Grease filters are cleaned weekly. Complaints about odour are tracked by the Hotel Directorate, and persistent sources are investigated.

## Industrial Emissions

Industrial processes, including the waste incinerators, battery charging bays, and clean rooms for electronics manufacturing, are regulated. Incinerator flue gas passes through scrubbers and filters before release. Battery charging bays are ventilated continuously to avoid hydrogen buildup, and lithium battery storage is isolated by fire zone. See `environmental_impact_and_ocean_ecology.md` for the full emissions profile.

## Clean Rooms

The electronics and pharmaceutical clean rooms (see `economics_and_trade.md`) use high-efficiency filtration and positive pressure. Entry is through airlocks, with protective clothing and showers. Clean rooms are monitored continuously for particle count.

## Staff

The ventilation system is maintained by about 900 staff in the Mechanical Services section of the Maintenance Department (see `daily_life_and_routines.md`). Their shifts are arranged so that at least one technician is on duty per terrace group at all times.
