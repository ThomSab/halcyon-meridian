# Automation and Robotics Aboard the Halcyon Meridian

## Why Automation Matters

The ship's population of 300,000 is large, but its operating crew is only about 38,000 (see `population_and_demographics.md`). That ratio is only possible because much of the ship's work is automated. Automation reduces staffing costs and improves safety in some areas, but it also creates new risks and new jobs in maintenance and supervision.

## Where Automation Is Used

| Area | Level of Automation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Sail control | High | Wingsails set automatically; crew override and monitor (see `sail_handling_and_sail_crew.md`) |
| Propulsion and power | High | Power management and load-sharing are automatic; operators supervise |
| Freight handling | High | Gantry cranes and about 250 driverless tractors (see `internal_transport.md`) |
| Water treatment | High | Reverse osmosis and membrane plants run with automated control (see `water_and_sanitation.md`) |
| Greenhouses and aquaculture | Medium to high | Climate, irrigation, and nutrient dosing are automated; staff handle harvest and fish |
| Waste collection | Medium | Night-time robots collect from terraces; staff sort and handle hazardous waste |
| Hull inspection | Medium | Remotely operated vehicles and crawlers; divers for detailed work (see `maintenance_refits_and_in_water_repair.md`) |
| Commissary | Low to medium | Some automated cooking and dishwashing; most food is prepared by people |
| Hospital | Low | Robotic supply delivery and pharmacy dispensing; surgery and nursing are done by people |

## Robots on the Spine

Small delivery robots carry parcels between the markets, the terraces, and the Harbour Hall. They use the Spine's walkways and marked lanes, and they are limited to 6 km/h in crowded areas. Robots are not allowed on the rail lines or in the elevators with passengers. Residents can see a robot's route and status on the intranet.

Cleaning robots work the terrace corridors overnight. They are monitored from the City Operations Centre (see `captain_and_command_structure.md`).

## Automation and Jobs

Automation has changed the crew profile. Fewer people are needed for routine operation, but more are needed for supervision, maintenance, and technical support. The Company has tried to avoid large redundancies by retraining workers and by moving staff into new roles as automation spreads.

The Residents' Council has asked for a formal review of any automation that removes more than 200 jobs in one department. The Board agreed to this in 2071 after a dispute over freight handling. Under the agreement, the Company must offer retraining and a transfer to another post before any redundancy.

## Safety and Failure

Automated systems can fail, and the ship's design assumes that they will. Key safeguards include:

- **Manual override** for all major systems, located on the Bridge or in the relevant department
- **Fail-safe modes** that stop machinery or hold it in a safe position
- **Regular testing** of automatic functions, including monthly drills in which operators take manual control
- **Logging** of all automatic decisions, so that incidents can be reviewed

Two incidents shaped current practice. In 2068, a faulty sensor caused a freight crane to stop mid-lift, dropping a container onto the cargo deck. No one was hurt, but the Board required every crane to have a secondary sensor. In 2070, a software update to the greenhouse climate controls changed the carbon dioxide dosing schedule, and crops in one greenhouse were lost before the error was found. Since then, all control software is tested on a mirror system before installation.

## Robot Standards

Robots must meet the Company's safety standards, which are based on international industrial standards for machinery. Any robot working near people must stop when someone enters its zone, and must be fitted with a physical bumper or a proximity sensor. Robots working outside the hull are tethered or controlled from the Bridge.

## Data and Oversight

Automated systems generate large amounts of data, which is stored on the ship's network and reviewed by the Operations Department. This data also supports the Company's planning and its reporting to the Board (see `surveillance_privacy_and_data.md`).

## Future Plans

The Engineering Department is studying more automation for the reactors' monitoring systems and for the sail maintenance platforms. Any system that affects reactor safety must be approved by the flag state, and the Company does not expect changes to reactor control to be made quickly.
