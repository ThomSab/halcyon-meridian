# Animals and Pets Aboard the Halcyon Meridian

## Overview

The ship is a closed environment with limited space, strict biosecurity, and a food system that depends on raising animals in controlled conditions. Animals aboard fall into three groups: livestock and farmed animals, companion animals, and wildlife that arrives with the ship or through port calls. Each group has its own rules.

## Livestock and Farmed Animals

The ship raises poultry on Deck 5 and fish in recirculating aquaculture tanks on Decks 6 to 8 (see `food_and_agriculture.md`). It also rears black soldier fly larvae and mealworms for protein (see `solid_waste_and_recycling.md`).

**Poultry.** The flock is about 9,000 birds, mostly chickens and some ducks. The poultry house is kept at controlled temperature and humidity, and birds are fed on insect meal and Commissary waste. Eggs are collected daily. Birds are slaughtered in a licensed facility on Deck 5, and the Medical Office inspects every carcass.

**Fish.** Tilapia and catfish are raised in closed tanks (see `food_and_agriculture.md`). Fish are not allowed to escape into the ocean, and the tank water is filtered and recirculated.

**Insects.** The insect rearing hall produces large quantities of larvae, which feed the poultry and fish. The hall is kept separate from other food areas, and its air is filtered (see `air_quality_and_ventilation.md`).

Livestock welfare is regulated by the Animal Welfare Office, which reports to the Chief Medical Officer. Standards include space per bird, temperature, lighting cycles, and humane slaughter. The office inspects the Deck 5 facilities each month.

## Companion Animals

Residents may keep companion animals, but only under licence. Permitted animals are:

| Animal | Permitted | Conditions |
|---|---|---|
| Fish in home aquaria | Yes | Up to 200 litres, with filtration and no ocean discharge |
| Small birds (such as finches and canaries) | Yes | Single cage, licensed, with health checks |
| Small reptiles (such as geckos) | Yes | Vivarium, licensed, no export |
| Rodents (such as hamsters and gerbils) | Yes | Cage, licensed, with health checks |
| Cats and dogs | No | Excluded; see below |
| Parrots and other large birds | No | Excluded |
| Exotic species | No | Excluded, under biosecurity and wildlife law |

Cats and dogs are excluded for several reasons. They need space to exercise, which the terraces do not provide. Their waste adds to the solid waste load. Their presence raises allergy and hygiene concerns in shared spaces. And the ship's quarantine rules make it hard to keep animals healthy and to control disease if an outbreak occurs.

Residents who had pets before joining may apply for an exemption, which the Animal Welfare Office assesses individually. Exemptions are rare and carry strict conditions.

## Licensing

Licences are issued by the Animal Welfare Office, and they cost about 40 LC per year. Applicants must show that the animal has a clean health record from port, that they can house and feed it, and that they accept the Company's rules on care and disposal. Animals must be microchipped and registered.

When a resident leaves the ship, the animal must leave with them or be rehomed through the Animal Welfare Office. Animals may not be landed at port without the port's own import approval.

## Biosecurity

Biosecurity is a serious concern. A disease introduced to the ship's livestock or pets could spread quickly. The Company's controls include:

- Health checks on all animals joining the ship, including those brought in by residents
- A quarantine period of 30 days for new animals
- Restrictions on the movement of animals between decks
- Monitoring of the poultry flock for avian influenza and other diseases
- Cleaning and disinfection of animal areas each day

If an outbreak is suspected, the affected area is isolated, and the Chief Medical Officer decides on the response. The Company's emergency plan includes culling, which is carried out humanely under the Animal Welfare Office's guidance.

## Wildlife on Board

Wildlife arrives on the ship in several ways. Birds sometimes land on the superstructure and the masts, particularly near land. Insects and rodents can come aboard with cargo or stores. Some seabirds follow the ship for days, feeding on waste from the outlets.

The Company does not feed wildlife. Rodent control is carried out by the Pest Control Team, which uses traps, sealed storage, and monitoring. Chemical controls are used sparingly, to avoid harm to food stocks and to the ocean.

## Pollinators

The greenhouses use bumblebees to pollinate tomatoes and peppers, because many of the plants do not self-pollinate well in enclosed spaces. The bees are raised in a licensed colony in the greenhouse hall, and they are monitored for disease. The Company keeps a small number of honeybee hives on the Solarium deck, which produce a modest amount of honey for the Commissary.

## Working Animals

The ship has no working animals. Dogs were considered for security and detection work, but the Company decided against them because of the restrictions on companion animals and the difficulty of exercising dogs aboard.

## Animal Welfare Complaints

Residents may report concerns about the treatment of animals to the Animal Welfare Office. Complaints are investigated, and the Office's findings are published in the annual report. The Office has a particular focus on the poultry flock, because it is the largest group of animals on the ship.

## Ethics and Debate

The Company's use of farmed animals is debated among residents. Some residents are vegetarian or vegan, and some object to the poultry and fish farming on ethical grounds. The Company respects these views, and it provides plant-based options in the Commissary (see `cuisine_and_regional_foods.md`). The Residents' Council has asked for a review of the poultry programme, which is due in the next budget cycle.
