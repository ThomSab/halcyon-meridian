# Immigration, Citizenship, and Visitors Aboard the Halcyon Meridian

## Nationality and the Flag

The ship is registered under the flag of a small island nation (see `governance_and_law.md`). Children born aboard receive that nationality under the registry's law. Adults who join the ship keep the nationality of their home country, and the ship does not grant citizenship to anyone.

This creates a practical problem: many residents are not citizens of the flag state and cannot easily claim its protections on shore. The Company provides a resident identity document, which is recognised by the flag state and by many port authorities, but it is not a passport.

## Entry to the Ship

People join the ship at port calls (see `ports_of_call.md`). The steps are:

1. Application for a contract or residence, made through the Company's recruitment office
2. Medical and background checks, carried out by the port medical service and the Company
3. Confirmation of housing grade and contract terms
4. Immigration check by the port authority, which may require a visa
5. Transfer by tender to the ship, with the Master's registration on arrival

Contract workers must hold a valid work permit for the port state if they are working ashore, and the Company must confirm this with the port authority. Residents without work permits are not permitted to join the ship from ports where a permit is required.

## Leaving the Ship

Residents may leave at any port call, subject to the terms of their contract and the rules in `population_and_demographics.md`. The Company requires a notice period for critical roles. Residents who owe money to the ship must settle their account before departure. The Master records the departure in the register.

## Statelessness

Some residents arrive without a valid passport, or with documents that port authorities do not accept. The Company works with the flag state and with the United Nations to resolve these cases. Residents in this position are issued a ship identity document and are not deported, because there is nowhere for the ship to send them mid-ocean.

## Visitors

The ship receives visitors. The Company allows **guest berths** for short stays of up to 14 days, with a cap of 500 guest berths at any time. Guests pay a fee, which covers their housing and meals, and they must comply with the Ship's Regulations.

Guest berths are sold through the Company's hospitality office, and they are used by:

- Family members visiting residents
- Business visitors, such as buyers, engineers, and auditors
- Researchers and journalists, with specific permissions (see `research_and_marine_science.md` and `arts_media_and_entertainment.md`)
- Tourists on short segments of the route, mainly in the Caribbean stretch

Visitors must register on arrival, and their visa status is checked by the port authority before they board.

## Visitor Restrictions

Visitors are not allowed in the reactor citadels, the Bridge, the Sail Control Room, the City Operations Centre, or the freight yard, unless they have a specific permit. They may use the Spine, the market halls, the Solarium, and the community halls, and they may attend public events.

Visitors are not allowed to vote in Council elections (see `elections_and_political_groupings.md`), and they cannot use the Company's welfare schemes or the Meridian Credit Union.

## Border Control at Sea

Border control is not carried out at sea, because the ship is outside any national jurisdiction. The Company's registration rules apply instead. In practice, this means:

- No formal border crossing takes place while the ship is at sea
- Immigration checks take place at port calls, under the authority of the port state
- The ship's registry records all people aboard

## Stowaways and Unauthorised Entry

Stowaways are handled by the Security Detachment and the Wardens (see `piracy_security_and_threats.md`). They are held in a separate room, assessed by the Medical Office, and referred to the port authority at the next call. The Company has a duty to provide humane treatment and medical care, and it does not hold stowaways for longer than necessary.

## Asylum and Protection

If a resident seeks asylum, the matter is referred to the port state at the next call, because the ship cannot grant asylum. The Company provides support during the process, including legal advice from the Maritime Court's panel of lawyers and interpretation services. The Articles prohibit the Company from returning anyone to a country where they face serious harm.

## Work Permits and Contracts

Residents who work in ports as part of the ship's operations (for example, on lighterage or port services) need work permits from the port state. The Company's legal team maintains the permit register and checks permits before each call.

## Records

The ship's registry keeps records of every person who joins, leaves, or is born aboard. These records are used for immigration checks, welfare claims, and the census. Records are kept for 50 years, and residents may request copies of their own entries.
