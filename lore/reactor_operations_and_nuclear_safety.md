# Reactor Operations and Nuclear Safety Aboard the Halcyon Meridian

## Overview

The ship's electrical power and most of its propulsion come from **four small modular reactors (SMRs)**, each rated at 160 MWe, for a total of 640 MWe (see `propulsion_and_power.md`). The reactors are pressurised water designs, housed in shielded citadels in the machinery spaces below the waterline. They are operated by the Engineering Department, which reports to the Chief Engineer.

The Chief Engineer holds the nuclear licence required by the flag state, and is responsible for reactor safety, licensing compliance, and reporting to the registry.

## The Citadels

Each reactor sits in its own citadel, a reinforced steel and concrete enclosure with:

- **Primary containment**, a sealed vessel around the reactor and its primary cooling loop
- **Secondary containment**, a second enclosure that can hold leaked radioactive material
- **Biological shielding**, thick concrete walls that reduce radiation outside the citadel
- **Independent cooling loops**, which reject heat to seawater through closed-loop heat exchangers

The citadels are arranged in pairs along the centreline, with a shielded control room for each pair. Access is restricted, and each entry requires a dosimeter and a logged permit.

## Operating Crew

The reactor operating crew numbers about **240 staff**, organised in four watches. Each watch has:

- A Shift Supervisor, holding a senior operator licence
- Two reactor operators, one per citadel pair
- A radiation protection technician
- A duty engineer on call

Operators train on a full-scope simulator, which is kept in the Engineering building on Deck 6. Each operator completes a recertification course every two years, and passes a practical examination on emergency procedures every year.

## Routine Operations

The reactors normally run at high capacity, and power is adjusted to meet demand. The average load is about 480 MW (see `propulsion_and_power.md`). Output is changed gradually, because large rapid changes stress the reactor and its turbines. Propulsion is controlled from the Bridge, and the power plant follows the ship's speed.

Each reactor is monitored continuously. The control room records temperature, pressure, neutron flux, coolant chemistry, and radiation levels, and the data is sent to the flag registry every hour.

## Refuelling

Each reactor is refuelled every **five to seven years**. Refuelling requires:

1. A port with licensed nuclear handling facilities (see `ports_of_call.md`)
2. Shutdown of the reactor and removal of the fuel assemblies
3. Transfer of spent fuel to a licensed carrier
4. Installation of fresh fuel and a full pressure test

The first refuelling took place in 2071 (see `history_and_construction_timeline.md`). The ship's refuelling schedule is planned years in advance and is one of the constraints on its route.

## Spent Fuel

Spent fuel is first cooled in a storage pool in the citadel, typically for several years, before it is removed. The pool has its own cooling system and backup power. Removal is carried out by a licensed carrier, under international rules for transport of radioactive material.

See `environmental_impact_and_ocean_ecology.md` for the Company's position on spent fuel and waste.

## Safety Systems

The reactors are protected by several layers of safety:

- **Automatic shutdown** if temperature, pressure, or neutron flux leave their safe ranges
- **Passive cooling** that can remove decay heat without pumps or power, using natural circulation
- **Emergency cooling injection** from stored water in tanks above the reactor
- **Redundant power supplies** for the control systems, including the battery bank and diesel generators (see `propulsion_and_power.md`)
- **Independent safety monitoring**, which reports directly to the Master and the flag state

## Emergency Drills

Reactor emergency drills are held twice a year, with a representative of the flag state observing. Scenarios include loss of coolant, loss of power, fire in a citadel, and flooding. Drill results are reviewed by the Chief Engineer and the Board.

## Radiation Protection

Radiation exposure is kept as low as reasonably achievable. Crew working near the citadels wear dosimeters, and their doses are recorded. The annual dose limit for radiation workers is well below the international limit, and the Company's own target is set lower still. The radiation protection team also monitors areas around the ship, including the hospital's imaging suite (see `hospital_and_medical_care.md`).

## Public Information

Residents are told about the reactors during induction and can read the Chief Engineer's quarterly safety report on the intranet. The report covers operating performance, drill results, and any events that were reportable to the flag state. In 2073, the Company reported one minor event, a small leak in a sampling line that was isolated within an hour and posed no risk to crew or residents.

## Staffing Pipeline

Nuclear operators come from three routes: the Company's own training school, transfers from the navy or commercial nuclear fleets, and promotion from the Engineering Department after the required courses. The Company tries to ensure that at least a third of operators have been trained aboard, so that knowledge stays on the ship.
