# Internal Transport Aboard the Halcyon Meridian

## Overview

Moving 300,000 people through a 1.6 km ship requires several transport systems working together. The main systems are the Spine rail lines, about 600 elevators, escalators and walkways, and the freight network in the holds. Residents use the Spine and elevators daily. Freight moves largely at night.

## Spine Rail

Two electric rail lines run the length of the Spine on Deck 9, one in each direction, with about **4 km of track** in total. Trains run at a maximum speed of 25 km/h, giving a full end-to-end trip of about **4 minutes**. The rail system carries roughly 180,000 passenger trips per day at peak service.

Each train has 6 to 8 cars, with level boarding platforms every 300 m. Trains run every 90 seconds at peak. Fares are not charged; the cost is included in the resident fee (see `economics_and_trade.md`).

Rail is powered by the ship's medium-voltage ring, which means a power fault in one zone may stop trains in that zone while the rest of the system continues. Trains are fitted with battery buffers for short outages.

## Elevators

About **600 elevators** serve the ship. Most are in banks at the terrace cores, and they travel at up to **5 m/s**, limited by stability and comfort. Typical waiting time at peak is under 90 seconds. Elevators are fitted with emergency brakes and cabin handrails, and they can be controlled from the City Operations Centre (see `captain_and_command_structure.md`).

Freight elevators are larger and slower, at up to 2 m/s, and are used for goods and stores between the holds and the Spine markets.

## Escalators and Walkways

Escalators link the Spine with the Solarium deck and the lower Spine concourse. Moving walkways run along the Spine's central axis at about 1 m/s. Walkways are not used in heavy weather, because ship motion makes them hazardous.

## Walking and Cycling

The Spine is the main walking route. Walking the full length takes about 25 minutes at a normal pace. Electric cycles are permitted on the Spine's outer lanes, with a maximum speed of 15 km/h and a licence requirement for minors. Cycling inside the terraces is prohibited.

## Freight Network

The freight network is separate from the passenger system. It consists of:
- **Cargo cranes**: 12 gantry cranes on the main cargo deck, lifting containers to and from holds
- **Automated guided vehicles (AGVs)**: about 250 driverless tractors, moving containers between the holds, the freight yard, and the Harbour Hall
- **Freight elevators**: about 40, linking the holds to the decks above
- **Freight yard**: the main cargo deck, which holds up to 12,000 TEU when fully loaded

Freight moves mainly during night hours to reduce conflict with passenger traffic. Cargo handling is supervised by the Cargo Department, which employs about 6,500 staff (see `daily_life_and_routines.md`).

## Movement in Storms

When Storm Law is declared (see `governance_and_law.md`), passenger movement is limited to designated routes. Trains may run at reduced speed, and elevators are restricted to the central banks. During Storm Subdivision, the watertight doors between the towers and the holds are closed, and freight movement stops entirely.

## Accidents and Safety

Serious accidents are rare, but they happen. The most common injuries are falls on escalators and crush injuries in freight areas. The Transport Safety Board investigates every incident involving injury, and it reports its findings to the Master.

In 2070, a freight elevator failed while loaded, injuring six crew. The cause was a worn brake pad that had passed inspection. The Board revised the inspection schedule and now requires a brake test on every freight elevator every 30 days.
