# Governance and Law Aboard the Halcyon Meridian

## Legal Status

The ship is registered under the flag of a small island nation, through an open maritime registry. This is common for large commercial vessels and is called a "flag of convenience." The flag state has jurisdiction over the ship on the high seas, and it issues the ship's registration certificate, safety certificates, and crew certificates.

The ship has no citizenship. Residents are not citizens of the flag state by default, but children born aboard receive the nationality of the flag state under the registry's law. Residents who want other nationalities must apply through their home country's consulate at port.

## Sources of Law

Four sources govern life aboard, in rough order of precedence:

1. **International maritime law.** The UN Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS), SOLAS, MARPOL (pollution), and STCW (crew training).
2. **The flag state's maritime code**, which implements international law.
3. **The Articles of Agreement**, the Company's charter. This sets out the Board's authority, the Master's powers, the rights of residents, and the rules for elections and courts.
4. **Ship's regulations**, issued by the Master and the Board, covering daily matters such as curfews, housing, and commissary rules.

## The Articles of Agreement

The Articles are the ship's constitution. They were written before launch and ratified by the initial investors and the first resident cohort. Key provisions include:

- Residents have the right to a hearing within 48 hours of any detention
- Residents have the right to counsel
- Residents may vote for members of the Residents' Council
- The Board may not reduce the free water allowance without a referendum
- The Master may not order any resident into hazardous work beyond their contract without consent, except in emergencies

Amendments require a two-thirds vote of the Board and a majority of the Residents' Council.

## The Residents' Council

The Council has **60 seats**, elected by ward every three years. Each ward covers roughly 4,400 residents (about 262,000 residents across 60 wards). Elections are held on the ship's network, with paper ballots as a backup. Turnout typically runs between 55 and 70 percent.

The Council has four main powers:
- It ratifies the annual budget for resident services (housing, food, water, schools)
- It can call a referendum on food and water price changes
- It can refer complaints about the Master or the Board to the Maritime Court
- It elects a liaison to meet the Master weekly, usually in the Solarium deck office

The Council cannot overrule the Master on navigation or safety. It cannot overrule the Board on commercial decisions unless a referendum passes, and then only by a two-thirds Board vote.

## The Board and the Master

The Board of Directors is the highest authority in business matters. It meets quarterly ashore, and it can communicate with the ship by satellite at any time. The Board appoints and dismisses the Master, approves the budget, and sets freight and resident charges.

The Master has sole authority over the ship's navigation and safety. Under maritime law, the Master's decisions on safety are final during a voyage. The Board may review them afterward.

## Storm Law

In an emergency, the Master may declare **Storm Law**. This temporarily:
- Restricts movement between decks to designated routes
- Suspends non-essential work outside the storm response
- Closes public venues and markets
- Requires residents to remain in their cabins or designated shelters

Storm Law can last up to 72 hours without Council approval. Any extension requires a vote of the Council.

## Courts

The **Maritime Court** is a three-judge panel aboard the ship. Judges are appointed by the Board from a panel nominated by the Residents' Council, and they serve five-year terms. The court hears civil disputes, labor disputes, and minor criminal cases.

Serious crimes, including murder, rape, and major fraud, are handled through a process called **handover**. The Master holds the accused in detention until the ship reaches a port, where the flag state or port state takes custody. This is rarely done quickly, and the Articles require the Master to file a detailed report within 24 hours.

Punishments aboard are limited to fines, loss of privileges, restricted movement, and detention for up to 90 days.

## Policing

The **Wardens** are the ship's police force, with about 2,500 officers. They are trained in maritime law, first aid, and crowd control. They patrol the terraces, the Spine, and the holds, and they operate the detention block of 300 cells.

Most common offenses are theft, assault, and violation of the water and food rules. Drug offenses are handled through the Medical Office when possible, and alcohol is restricted to licensed venues.

## Labor Rights

Crew members are covered by the Articles and the STCW code. Crew are not permitted to strike during a voyage, but they may file grievances with the Maritime Court and request mediation through the Residents' Council. Mutiny, which includes any organized refusal to obey a lawful order that endangers the ship, is a serious crime under maritime law.

## Marriages, Births, and Deaths

The Master may witness and certify marriages, births, and deaths for the ship's registry. These certificates are recognized by the flag state. Divorce is handled by the Maritime Court. Burials at sea are conducted in accordance with the Articles and international guidelines on environmental impact.

## Conflicts Between Authorities

The most common conflicts are:
- **Board versus Master.** The Board may prioritize revenue; the Master may prioritize safety. Under the Articles, safety decisions by the Master are binding during a voyage.
- **Board versus Council.** The Council may push for lower rents or higher food subsidies; the Board may resist for financial reasons. Referenda are the formal resolution mechanism.
- **Residents versus Wardens.** Trust is a recurring issue, particularly after detentions. The Articles provide hearing rights, and the Maritime Court reviews complaints.
