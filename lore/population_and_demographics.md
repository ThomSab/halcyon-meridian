# Population and Demographics of the Halcyon Meridian

## Overview

The ship's population is about 300,000 people, made up of two groups:

- **Crew and operations:** about 38,000. These are employed by the Company in roles such as engineering, navigation, and security. Crew live in dedicated crew blocks, and some have family members who live with them as residents.
- **Residents:** about 262,000. These are people who live aboard under contract or as family members of a worker, including children and retirees.

## Age Structure of Residents

| Age Group | Number | Share |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 4 | 14,000 | 5% |
| 5 to 16 (compulsory schooling) | 36,000 | 14% |
| 17 | 8,000 | 3% |
| 18 to 64 | 170,000 | 65% |
| 65 and over | 34,000 | 13% |

Children under 18 total about 58,000. The share of residents aged 65 and over is higher than in most port cities because many retirees choose to live aboard, where housing and care are included in their contracts.

## Births, Deaths, and the Ceiling

The Demographic Office licenses births. In a typical year, between **1,600 and 2,200** births are approved, and about **1,400** deaths occur, mostly among older residents. Net growth is therefore around 200 to 800 people per year.

The Articles of Agreement set a population ceiling of **310,000**, adopted by amendment in 2074 (see `history_and_construction_timeline.md`). The ceiling reflects the capacity of water production, food supply, and housing. At current growth rates, the ceiling would be reached within about 15 years. When the population approaches the ceiling, the Board reduces the number of licensed births. The Residents' Council has argued that this should be decided by referendum, but the Articles allow the Board to set birth licenses by a two-thirds vote.

## Birth Licensing

A couple may apply for a birth licence once their housing grade and income meet the Demographic Office's criteria. The application is reviewed by a panel of three officers, including one from the Medical Office. Approval is not guaranteed. Births outside the licence system are rare, and the Office does not penalize them directly, but the child's housing and schooling allocation is reduced until the family meets the criteria.

Children born aboard receive the nationality of the flag state (see `governance_and_law.md`) and are registered by the Master.

## Migration

Net migration is small, typically within about 2,000 people per year in either direction. People join the ship at port calls: contract workers arrive to fill vacancies, and residents depart if they have finished their contracts or have family emergencies. Departure requires paying any outstanding ledger balance and passage costs (see `economics_and_trade.md`).

## Deaths and Burials

Deaths aboard are certified by the Master and the Chief Medical Officer. The ship has a cold room capacity of about 300 bodies. Bodies are returned to port or buried at sea under the procedures described in `elder_care_and_end_of_life.md`.

## Sex and Household Structure

About 52 percent of residents are women. Around 38 percent of households are single-person households, most of them in the Grade 2 and Grade 3 housing (see `housing_and_residential_terraces.md`). Family households are most common in the Grade 0 suites.

## Demographic Research

The Demographic Office publishes an annual report. It tracks fertility, mortality, mental health indicators, and the number of residents who leave the ship at each port. Its most cited finding is that residents who spend more than three years aboard are more likely to report sleep disorders than those who have been aboard for less than one year, which the Office attributes to long-term exposure to vibration and motion.
