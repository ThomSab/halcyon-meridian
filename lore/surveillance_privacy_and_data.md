# Surveillance, Privacy, and Data Aboard the Halcyon Meridian

## The Trade-off

A closed ship with 300,000 people produces a lot of data. Sensors monitor air, water, power, and structure (see `air_quality_and_ventilation.md` and `ship_structure_and_layout.md`), and the ledger records every transaction. Some of this data is needed for safety. Some supports the Company's business. Some could be used to watch people. The ship's governance has to decide where the lines are.

The Articles of Agreement guarantee privacy "to the extent consistent with the safety of the vessel and the lawful functions of the Company" (see `governance_and_law.md`). Much of the rest of this file is about how that balance is drawn.

## What Is Collected

| Category | Source | Retention | Who Can Access |
|---|---|---|---|
| Environmental sensors | Air, water, power, and structure sensors | 5 years | Engineering, Hotel Directorate, Medical Office |
| Ledger transactions | Wages, rent, purchases, and charges | 10 years | Accounts, Auditors, the resident, Wardens with warrant |
| Medical records | Hospital and clinics | As required by health law; minimum 25 years | Medical staff and the resident |
| Cabin access logs | Door locks and biometric readers | 90 days | Wardens with warrant, Hotel Directorate for repairs |
| Public camera footage | Spine, transport, and external decks | 30 days | Wardens and the Maritime Court |
| Intranet messages | Residents' messaging | 1 year, unless deleted | Restricted; see below |
| Vehicle and robot logs | Transport, freight, and cleaning robots | 2 years | Operations, Transport Safety Board |

## Cameras

Public spaces are covered by about 6,000 cameras. The Spine, the rail platforms, the market halls, the elevators, and the holds have the most coverage. Cameras are not placed inside cabins, bathrooms, or medical treatment rooms.

Footage is held for 30 days and is accessed only with a Warden's logged request and, for anything beyond routine incident review, the approval of the Chief Warden. Residents may see footage of themselves on request, subject to the rights of others who appear in it.

## Access Control

Doors are controlled by an access system that uses cards or biometric readers. Each entry is logged. The logs are used to find people in emergencies, support muster checks (see `emergency_response_and_evacuation.md`), and investigate offences (see `crime_and_justice_in_practice.md`).

The Articles require that access logs for residential doors are not used for routine monitoring of residents' movements. They may be used in an investigation, with a warrant from the Maritime Court or in a declared emergency.

## Messaging and Monitoring

The intranet is not monitored in real time. Messages are stored, but they are not read by staff unless one of the following applies:

- A Maritime Court warrant has been issued
- A safety threat has been reported and the Chief Warden has authorised a review
- A resident has requested a welfare check, which is logged

Automated filters block messages containing known threats, such as links to malicious software, and they flag messages that suggest self-harm or violence. Flagged messages are reviewed by a small team of trained staff, who report to the Medical Office or the Chief Warden, depending on the concern.

## Ledger Data

The ledger holds the full financial record of every resident and of the Company. Accounts can be audited by the Company's internal audit team, the external auditors, and the Maritime Court. Residents can view their own account history, and they can request a statement once a month without charge.

The Market Authority uses ledger data to detect price fixing and fraud. It does not use the data to target individual residents.

## Health Data

Health records are held under strict rules. Only staff directly involved in a resident's care can view their record. Records can be shared with port medical services if the resident consents. In an emergency, the Chief Medical Officer may authorise sharing without consent, and the authorisation is logged and reported to the Master.

## Data Sales

The Company sells ocean and weather data to clients (see `economics_and_trade.md`). Resident data is not sold. Aggregate data about resident health, housing, and population may be shared with research bodies in anonymised form, after review by the Demographic Office.

## Sensitive Groups

The Articles give particular protection to children, victims of crime, and residents who have requested confidentiality on health or religious matters. Data about children is limited to what is needed for school, health, and welfare. Chaplains and counsellors do not share confidential conversations (see `religion_and_chaplaincy.md`).

## Oversight

Data use is overseen by a Privacy Committee of five people: two residents elected by the Residents' Council, two Board nominees, and a chair appointed by the Maritime Court. The Committee reviews any new surveillance system before it is installed and publishes an annual report.

## Complaints

Residents can complain to the Privacy Committee, the Maritime Court, or the Council's ombudsman. Complaints about the Wardens are investigated by the Maritime Court. Complaints about the Company's data use go to the Privacy Committee first.

## Known Disputes

The most contested issue has been the 2071 proposal to add microphones to the Spine for crowd-noise sensing, which would have helped detect fights and fires. The Council rejected the proposal after a referendum, and the Company installed visual sensors instead. The vote was 58 percent against.
