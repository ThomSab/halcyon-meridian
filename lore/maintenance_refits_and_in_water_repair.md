# Maintenance, Refits, and In-Water Repair Aboard the Halcyon Meridian

## The Basic Problem

A ship of this size cannot be lifted into a conventional dry dock. The main dry basin used for construction, at Ferrol (see `history_and_construction_timeline.md`), is the only facility the Company has found that could take the hull, and it is not available for routine work. Maintenance is therefore carried out mostly **in water**, while the ship is at anchor or at sea.

## Maintenance Department

The Maintenance Department has about **7,000 staff** (see `daily_life_and_routines.md`). It is divided into:

- **Hull and structure**: inspections, plate repair, coating, and corrosion control
- **Mechanical services**: pumps, valves, fans, and the water and waste plants
- **Electrical and instrumentation**: power distribution, sensors, and controls
- **Terrace and facilities**: housing repairs, lifts, and public spaces

Each section has its own workshops, which are located on the decks where their work is needed. Routine repairs are logged in the maintenance system, which tracks parts, labour, and the age of every major component.

## Hull Inspection

The hull is inspected continuously and in detail on a fixed schedule:

| Inspection | Frequency | Method |
|---|---|---|
| Visual survey of above-water hull | Monthly | Crew with cameras and drones |
| Underwater hull survey | Every three months | Divers and remotely operated vehicles (ROVs) |
| Ultrasonic thickness survey | Annually | Divers and automated crawlers |
| Keel and structural survey | Every five years | Full underwater and internal survey |
| Major refit | Every seven years | Purpose-built basin, with cofferdams (see below) |

Inspection data is stored in the hull digital twin, a computer model of the hull that tracks plate thickness, weld condition, and cracking. The model also uses the strain sensors along the keel (see `ship_structure_and_layout.md`) to estimate fatigue life.

## Cleaning and Antifouling

Marine growth on the hull increases drag and fuel use. The hull uses a biocide-free silicone coating (see `environmental_impact_and_ocean_ecology.md`), which sheds growth at speed. At low speed or at anchor, growth can build up, and divers or robots remove it during routine cleaning.

Cleaning is done with brushes and high-pressure water, and the waste is collected by a suction system to prevent debris entering the sea. The Company's environmental team checks each cleaning operation.

## In-Water Repairs

Small repairs are carried out in water using:

- **Divers**, from the Underwater Services team of about 60 people, working in pairs with surface support
- **ROVs**, which can cut, weld, and inspect at depths down to 60 m
- **Underwater welding**, which is used only for temporary repairs, because wet welding is weaker than dry welding

Divers work to strict depth and time limits, and each dive is logged. The Underwater Services team reports to the Chief Engineer and follows the same safety rules as other industries that work underwater.

## Cofferdam Work

For major repairs to the hull, the ship must be partly dried out. The Company uses a system of **cofferdams**, which are temporary enclosures fixed to the hull over the area to be repaired. Water is pumped out of the cofferdam, and the work is carried out in the dry.

Cofferdams are used for propeller pod and rudder work, sections of the double bottom, and bottom plating. Each cofferdam is installed in a few days and removed when the work is complete. The ship must be in calm water for cofferdam work, which limits when it can be done.

## Major Refits

Every seven years, the ship undergoes a major refit in a purpose-built basin. The refit includes:

- Full hull inspection and repair, including cofferdam work across the bottom
- Replacement of worn shafts, bearings, and pod components
- Overhaul of the sail masts and their drive systems (see `sail_handling_and_sail_crew.md`)
- Refuelling of the reactors, if due (see `reactor_operations_and_nuclear_safety.md`)
- Upgrades to living quarters, farms, and industrial plant

A refit takes about **five months**. During this period, the population is either moved to other vessels, reduced by a planned resident departure, or kept aboard with reduced services. The 2072 refit was the first, and it took five months and cost about 1.4 billion ledger credits equivalent (see `economics_and_trade.md`).

Only a few basins in the world can take the ship for a refit. The Company has a contract with one yard, and a second is on standby.

## Spares and Stores

The Company holds about 60,000 spare parts in the holds, including bearings, valves, pumps, cable, and sail components. The most valuable are the pod thruster spares, which are kept in dry storage in a separate hold. The spares inventory is audited monthly, and the Maintenance Department reports stock levels to the Chief Operating Officer.

## Staff Safety

Maintenance is one of the most hazardous areas of work on the ship. The most common injuries are falls from height, burns from welding, and crush injuries from heavy parts. The Maintenance Department runs its own safety committee, and every serious incident is investigated by an independent team.

## Residents and Repair

Residents report faults in their cabins and public spaces through the terrace system (see `housing_and_residential_terraces.md`). The Hotel Directorate sends the request to the Maintenance Department, which schedules repairs. Persistent problems, such as leaks or noise, are escalated to the Chief Engineer.
