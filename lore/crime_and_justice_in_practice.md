# Crime and Justice in Practice Aboard the Halcyon Meridian

## Overview

This file describes how crime works in daily life aboard the ship. The legal structure (courts, Articles, and handover) is covered in `governance_and_law.md`. The focus here is on the offences that happen, how they are handled, and what the statistics look like.

## Recorded Offences

The Wardens record about **6,000 offences per year**, or about 2.3 per 100 residents. This is lower than many urban areas on shore, but the ship's closed environment and high density mean that small incidents are more visible.

| Offence Type | Share of Recorded Offences | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Theft | 34% | Mostly from cabins and storage lockers |
| Assault (minor) | 22% | Mostly during arguments, often involving alcohol |
| Breaches of the food and water rules | 14% | Hoarding, illegal trading, misuse of allowances |
| Fraud and ledger manipulation | 8% | Mostly by staff with access to accounts |
| Public order (noise, obstruction, unlicensed events) | 9% | Common in the Spine after dark |
| Drugs | 5% | Most cases are possession; trafficking is rare |
| Serious assault | 2% | Injury requiring hospital treatment |
| Other (including criminal damage) | 6% | |

Serious crimes, such as murder and rape, are very rare. The Wardens recorded three homicides in the first ten years of operation, and all three were referred for handover at the next port.

## How Incidents Are Handled

Most incidents follow this path:

1. **Report.** Incidents are reported through the terrace clinic, a Warden post, the intranet, or the City Operations Centre.
2. **Response.** A Warden attends. For medical emergencies, the Medical Office responds first.
3. **Investigation.** The Warden records the facts, takes statements, and checks any evidence. The Investigations Unit handles complex cases.
4. **Decision.** For minor offences, the Warden may issue a warning, a fine, or a restriction. For more serious matters, the case goes to the Maritime Court.
5. **Appeal.** Residents may appeal any penalty to the Maritime Court within 14 days.

Most minor cases are resolved within a day. Wardens are encouraged to use mediation, and terrace committees often help settle disputes between neighbours.

## Typical Penalties

| Penalty | Use |
|---|---|
| Warning | First minor offences |
| Fine | Up to 200 LC, most common penalty |
| Loss of privileges | Such as market access, cinema access, or Spine permits |
| Restricted movement | Limited to specific decks or terraces, up to 30 days |
| Detention | Up to 90 days, usually 1 to 7 days |
| Referral for handover | Serious crimes, held pending port custody |

Detention is used sparingly. The detention block has 300 cells, and the average detention is about three days. Detainees have a hearing within 48 hours, as the Articles require (see `governance_and_law.md`).

## Theft and Security of Property

Theft is the most common offence. The Wardens recommend that residents lock lockers and use the cabin security system, which logs entry and exit. The Hotel Directorate is responsible for locks on residential doors. Stolen property is often recovered in the markets, because the market licensing system tracks sales.

## Drugs

The Company treats drug use as a health issue wherever possible. Prescription medicines are tightly controlled through the pharmacy. Recreational drugs are illegal, and possession is an offence. Residents who are dependent may be referred to the Medical Office for treatment, and the Company's policy is to prefer treatment to punishment for personal use.

Alcohol is licensed and taxed. Public drunkenness is an offence, and the Wardens may take intoxicated people to the Medical Office to recover. Alcohol is banned in the nurseries and the hospital.

## Violence

Most assaults happen during arguments in the terraces, the Spine's bars, or the Aft Hall. The Wardens have a standard approach: separate the people involved, take statements, and assess injuries. Mediation is offered where both parties agree. Repeat offenders are referred to the Maritime Court.

Domestic violence is a serious offence and is treated as a priority. The Wardens and the Medical Office have a joint protocol, and the Maritime Court can issue protection orders that restrict contact.

## Fraud and Ledger Crime

Fraud is a growing concern because the ledger system holds most of the ship's money. Common frauds include falsified wage claims, manipulated housing allocations, and unauthorized transfers. The Company's audit team reviews transactions monthly, and staff with access to accounts are subject to background checks.

## Wardens

The Wardens are described in `governance_and_law.md`. Their training includes first aid, de-escalation, and maritime law. Wardens do not carry firearms, except for the armed response team, which is also used for piracy defence (see `piracy_security_and_threats.md`).

## Prisoners and Welfare

Detainees are held in conditions set by the Articles, with access to food, water, exercise, medical care, and visits from family and counsel. Visiting is allowed in the detention block's visiting room. Detainees are not forced to work, but they may choose to do supervised work for a small allowance.

## Public Perception

Residents' surveys show that most people feel safe in their terraces and on the Spine during the day. Fewer feel safe at night in the Aft Hall and the lower Spine concourse. The Wardens have increased patrols in those areas, and the Residents' Council has asked for more lighting (see `daylight_and_lighting.md`).
