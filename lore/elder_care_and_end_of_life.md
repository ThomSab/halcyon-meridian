# Elder Care and End of Life Aboard the Halcyon Meridian

## The Population

About **34,000 residents** are aged 65 or over, which is around 13 percent of the resident population (see `population_and_demographics.md`). Many retirees choose to live aboard because the ship provides housing, medical care, and social contact, and because their families may work on board.

The number of older residents is growing. The Demographic Office expects it to reach about 20 percent of residents within 20 years, which will shift demand toward care and away from schooling and new housing.

## The Care Complex

The **Harbour Care Complex** is a set of buildings at the aft end of the Spine on Deck 11. It includes:

- **Residential care**: 400 beds in single rooms with en-suite facilities
- **Dementia care**: 120 beds in a secure wing, with garden spaces designed to reduce wandering risk
- **Day care**: a centre for older residents who live in their own cabins and need help during the day
- **Rehabilitation**: physiotherapy and occupational therapy gyms
- **Palliative care**: a 30-bed unit for patients with terminal illness

The complex is staffed by about 500 people, including nurses, carers, physiotherapists, and social workers. Carers are trained in the Further Education Centre (see `schools_and_education.md`).

## Home Care

Most older residents live in their own cabins or in Harbour Terraces (see `housing_and_residential_terraces.md`). Home care visits are provided by the Medical Office's community nurses, who visit each resident at least once a day if needed. Meals can be delivered from the Commissary.

## Falls and Mobility

Falls are the most common cause of injury among older residents. Ship motion makes falls more likely. The Harbour Terraces have grab rails, non-slip flooring, and extra lighting in corridors. Residents who have fallen are reviewed by a physiotherapist before returning to normal activity.

## Dementia

Dementia care aboard focuses on routine and familiar surroundings. The ship's clock and the motion of the ship can both cause disorientation. The care complex uses clear signs, consistent staff, and light therapy (see `daylight_and_lighting.md`) to support residents' sleep and orientation.

Residents with dementia may be at risk of wandering into restricted areas, including the engineering spaces. The Chief Warden and the Medical Office work together to identify these risks, and access controls are set on relevant doors.

## Costs and Eligibility

Care is included in the contract for residents aged 65 and over who have lived aboard for at least five years. Residents who have not met this requirement may pay for care through the ledger, with a subsidy from the Company for lower-income households. Care costs are a significant share of the Company's operating budget, and the Board reviews them each year.

## Death Aboard

When a resident dies aboard, the Medical Office certifies the death, and the Master records it in the ship's register. The Chaplaincy is informed, and a chaplain is assigned to the family (see `religion_and_chaplaincy.md`). The body is stored in the cold room, which holds about 300 bodies. The ship does not have a crematorium.

## Funeral Options

Families have three options:

1. **Return to port.** The body is landed at the next port call and transferred to a funeral home or to a family member. This is the most common choice, and the Company pays the costs of transport if the family is in financial hardship.
2. **Burial at sea.** Permitted under international guidance, with the burial made at least 3 nautical miles from land, and in water deeper than 200 m. The Master conducts the service or delegates it to a chaplain. Burial at sea requires a weighted, biodegradable shroud and is recorded in the registry.
3. **Memorial service only.** The family holds a service aboard, and the remains are returned to port later.

Burials at sea are rare, about 15 to 20 a year, and most families choose port return.

## Palliative Care

The palliative unit is designed to be as comfortable as possible. Patients can have a window or a view of the Spine's skylights. Family may stay overnight. Pain relief is managed by a specialist team, and chaplains and counsellors are available to families.

## Advance Care Planning

Residents are encouraged to make an advance care plan, setting out their wishes for treatment and end-of-life care. Plans are stored in the health database (see `hospital_and_medical_care.md`) and are reviewed each year. Plans are binding within the limits of the Medical Office's standards.

## Staff Wellbeing

Care staff are at risk of burnout, particularly in dementia and palliative care. The Medical Office provides regular supervision, peer support, and time off after bereavements. Staff who have cared for a resident over several years are often offered a formal farewell session when they leave the complex.
