# Language, Culture, and Social Classes Aboard the Halcyon Meridian

## Languages

Residents speak more than 60 languages at home. The ship's working language is **English**, which is the international standard for maritime communication under the IMO's Standard Marine Communication Phrases. All officers, crew, and official notices use English, and children are taught it in school (see `schools_and_education.md`).

Everyday conversation on the Spine often uses **Meridian Common**, an English-based creole that developed in the first years of the voyage. It borrows from many languages, including Portuguese, Spanish, Tagalog, Hindi, Mandarin, Yoruba, and Arabic. Words like *sharp* (good quality), *dry* (for goods that don't spoil), and *hold* (a storage space) have taken on shipboard meanings that are unfamiliar on shore.

Residents who speak English as a second language are supported by the Language Office, which offers classes for adults and interpreters for medical, legal, and administrative matters.

## Cultures and Groups

Residents form communities based on home country, faith, trade, and terrace. The strongest identities are usually tied to the terrace, because residents of the same terrace share the same garden, laundry, and common room. Terrace committees (see `housing_and_residential_terraces.md`) are often the first point of contact for newcomers.

Other common groupings include:
- **Trade guilds**, formed by crew and workers in the same department, such as the Engineering Mutual and the Galley Association
- **Clubs and societies**, including sports clubs, language circles, and hobby groups
- **Family networks**, which are especially important for households that have members in different departments

## Social Classes

The ship has no formal class system, and the Articles prohibit discrimination based on origin or belief. In practice, however, there are clear differences in income, housing, and status. Four broad groups can be identified:

| Group | Typical Role | Housing | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Officers and senior staff** | Captain's team, department heads, senior engineers | Grade 0 suites, bridge deck quarters | Hold most positions of authority |
| **Skilled crew** | Engineers, nurses, navigators, technicians | Grade 1 or Grade 0 | Strong union-like associations |
| **Workers** | Cargo, farms, cleaning, catering | Grade 2 or Grade 3 | Largest group; most dependent on the Commissary |
| **Dependants and retirees** | Family members, children, older residents | Varies with the worker's grade | Often have the least income of their own |

Mobility between groups is possible. Many officers began as workers, and the Further Education Centre (see `schools_and_education.md`) offers routes into crew roles. However, the ship's structure means that people born into lower grades are more likely to stay there.

## Class and Consumption

Income differences show in everyday life. Grade 0 residents shop at the Central Hall's clothing shops and eat more often in the Aft Hall's restaurants. Grade 3 residents rely more on the Commissary and the Forward Hall's produce. The Market Authority reports that the gap between the highest and lowest income households is about 6 to 1.

## Status and Dress

Uniforms distinguish crew from residents. Officers wear navy uniforms with rank insignia on the shoulders. Engineers wear grey, deck crew wear dark green, and medical staff wear white. Residents dress as they choose, but the Company requires non-slip shoes in working areas and high-visibility vests on the external decks.

## Tensions

The most common social tensions are:
- **Housing allocation.** Residents who feel they have been overlooked for Grade 1 or Grade 0 housing sometimes raise complaints with the Residents' Council.
- **Crew privileges.** Some residents believe that crew receive better food and transport. The Company has published the Commissary's allowances for each group to reduce this suspicion.
- **Newcomers.** Residents who joined the ship at a later port may find that terrace networks are already established. The terrace committees run welcome sessions for new residents.

## Gender and Equality

Women make up about half of the crew and a little over half of residents. Women hold about a third of officer positions, and the Company publishes an annual gender pay report. The gap between men and women in equivalent roles is small, but women are less likely to reach the most senior positions, which the Company is working to address.

## Speech and Discussion

Public discussion is common in the Spine's community halls and the market halls. Open disagreement with officers is allowed, but insults, threats, and incitement to violence are offences under the Ship's Regulations (see `crime_and_justice_in_practice.md`).
