# Schools and Education Aboard the Halcyon Meridian

## Overview

Education is compulsory from age 5 to 16 under the Articles of Agreement. The ship has about **58,000 children** under 18, including 14,000 aged 0 to 4 who attend nurseries rather than school. About **36,000** children are of compulsory school age, and about **8,000** are 17 and attend further education.

There are about **2,400 teachers**, giving a ratio of roughly 15 pupils per teacher.

## Where Children Learn

Schooling takes place in two settings:

- **Dedicated schools** on decks 23 to 28, with nine full schools. These house about 40 percent of pupils.
- **Community classrooms** in the Spine's community halls and the common rooms of the residential terraces. These house the remaining 60 percent.

The dedicated schools have science laboratories, a library, a hydroponics classroom on the Solarium deck, and an engineering workshop. Community classrooms are simpler, usually with fold-down desks and shared screens.

## Curriculum

The Company curriculum has a fixed core and local options:

- **Core:** mathematics, science, reading and writing, and English as the working language of the ship
- **Maritime studies:** navigation basics, weather, ship systems, and seamanship, taught from age 9
- **Life skills:** cooking, first aid, water and food safety, and basic financial literacy using ledger credits
- **Languages:** the home language of the family, supported by the schools, plus a choice of a second language

Maritime studies are compulsory. They are the ship's main way of building future crew, and the Company's staffing plans depend on them.

## Language

The working language on the ship is English, which is the international standard for maritime communication. Most teaching is in English, with additional support for children whose first language is different. Teachers are trained to use English for technical terms, with the home language used for explanation.

## Port Schooling

During port calls, families may choose to send children to local schools for the duration of the call. Children who attend local schools are tracked through a transfer record, and the schools are required to give a report to the ship's education office. Most families keep their children aboard, but about 5 percent take up port schooling each year, mostly during long port calls.

## Attendance and Performance

Attendance averages about 94 percent. The most common absences are caused by seasickness in the first two weeks of a voyage and by illness during norovirus outbreaks. The Education Office publishes standardized test results each year. Performance is broadly similar to shore schools, with weaker results in reading among children who joined the ship after age 10.

## Teachers

Most teachers are on two-year contracts. They receive the same housing grade as crew with equivalent qualifications. Training is provided by the Education Office, and teachers must hold a recognized teaching qualification from their home country or pass the ship's certification course.

## Further Education

Students aged 17 and over may enter the Further Education Centre, on Deck 27. The centre offers vocational courses in engineering, hydroponics, medical support, catering, and navigation. Successful students can apply for crew positions, or for places on shore universities through a partnership scheme. The partnership is limited to about 200 places per year.

## Childcare

Nurseries for children aged 0 to 4 are run on the residential terraces, with one nursery for every two terraces. Staff are supervised by the Medical Office. Nursery places are allocated by ballot, and the waiting list is long.
