# Daily Life and Routines Aboard the Halcyon Meridian

## Ship Time

The ship keeps a single clock based on the time zone of its departure port. As it crosses longitudes, it adjusts by one hour at 03:00 each day. This is staggered so that not all systems change at once. Crossing the Atlantic (about 60 degrees of longitude) takes roughly four days of clock changes. Many residents report several days of disrupted sleep, a known effect that the Medical Office tracks as "transit fatigue."

Work shifts are referenced to ship time, not local time. The day is divided into three eight-hour shifts for most workers. Navigation and engineering watch-keepers follow the traditional merchant pattern of four hours on and eight hours off, rotating through the night.

## Work

Roughly 38,000 people are crew and operations staff. Their approximate distribution:

| Department | Staff |
|---|---|
| Maintenance and damage control | 7,000 |
| Food production and commissary | 6,500 |
| Cargo and logistics | 6,500 |
| Engineering and power | 6,000 |
| Deck and navigation | 3,500 |
| Security (Wardens) | 2,500 |
| Water and waste | 2,200 |
| Medical | 1,800 |
| Administration | 2,000 |

Most adult residents who are not crew hold Company jobs: working in greenhouses, schools, shops, clinics, laundries, or the freight yards. Work contracts usually run two years. A standard week is 40 hours.

## Housing and Sleep

Living space averages 15 m² per person. A single adult typically has a cabin of 9 to 12 m². A family of four usually shares 45 to 60 m². Cabins are on the terrace levels of the residential towers.

Sleep is affected by constant low-frequency vibration from the pod thrusters, roughly 1 to 2 Hz, and by ship motion. Typical roll is 3 to 5 degrees; in storms it can exceed 10 degrees. About one in ten new residents experiences seasickness in the first week, and many people adapt within two weeks. Cabins have sway-compensated bunks, a common fitting on long-haul vessels.

## Health

The ship has about 1,200 hospital beds, a surgical theatre complex, an imaging suite, and a dental clinic. Births are licensed by the Demographic Office, which allows roughly 1,600 to 2,200 births per year. Deaths average about 1,400 per year, mostly among older residents.

Common complaints include:
- Respiratory infections, which spread quickly in enclosed spaces
- Norovirus outbreaks, which are the most disruptive; infected residents are isolated in the Quarantine Ward
- Vitamin D deficiency, because people spend less time in sunlight; UV lamps are installed on residential decks
- Musculoskeletal problems from motion and sleep disruption

Mental health is a recognized concern. Long passages can produce what the Medical Office calls "horizon fatigue," characterized by withdrawal, insomnia, and low mood. Counsellors and a shipboard chaplaincy run regular sessions.

## Communications

Internet access is provided through low-Earth-orbit satellite links with an aggregate capacity of about 8 Gbps. Shared across 300,000 people, this works out to a few kilobits per second per person on average. Residents therefore rely on an offline intranet with messaging, news, school materials, and cached media. Video calls are limited to scheduled slots and are reserved for medical and legal use. Personal internet access is metered and priced in ledger credits (see `economics_and_trade.md`).

## Education

Schooling is compulsory from age 5 to 16. About 40 percent of children attend shipboard schools in the residential terraces; the rest attend classes held in the Spine's community halls. Schools run on ship time and follow the Company curriculum, which includes navigation, hydroponics, engineering basics, and maritime law. During port calls, children may attend classes on land if their families choose.

## Recreation

The Spine (see `ship_structure_and_layout.md`) contains a 1.6 km looped running track, called the Spine Run, on Deck 9. Athletic fields are on the Solarium deck, under translucent panels. Libraries, cinemas, cafés, and a small number of bars are located in the Spine's market halls. Alcohol is restricted to licensed venues and is taxed heavily. Religious services are held in multi-faith chapels on several decks.

## A Typical Day

- **06:00** Shift change for the food and water departments. Breakfast service opens in the commissary.
- **07:00** Morning commuters on the Spine rail lines. Schools open.
- **12:00** Lunch peak in the market halls.
- **18:00** Evening commute. Recreation venues open to full capacity.
- **22:00** Lights dim in residential decks. Night shift staff are at their posts. Wardens patrol the terraces.
- **03:00** Ship time clock adjustment, if one is scheduled.
