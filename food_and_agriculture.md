# Food and Agriculture Aboard the Halcyon Meridian

## Daily Requirement

A typical adult needs about 2,300 kilocalories per day. Children and manual workers need more. Across 300,000 people, the ship must supply roughly **700 million kcal per day**. Protein demand is about 60 grams per person per day, or roughly 18 tonnes of protein per day for the whole population.

The ship cannot grow this much food. The central challenge is that calories are cheap to buy in port and expensive to grow onboard, while fresh vitamins and fresh protein are hard to store.

## Sources of Food

| Source | Share of Calories | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Dry staples (rice, wheat, oats, dried pulses, oils) | ~70% | Bought in port, stored in dry holds |
| Chilled and frozen proteins (meat, dairy, fish) | ~12% | Bought in port; limited by cold storage |
| Recirculating aquaculture (tilapia, catfish) | ~7% | Roughly 20% of onboard protein |
| Greenhouse vegetables, herbs, sprouts | ~5% | Most fresh produce; key source of vitamin C |
| Insects (black soldier fly, mealworm) | ~3% | Protein for people and feed for fish |
| Microalgae (spirulina, chlorella) | ~2% | Dense protein, B12, iron |
| Poultry and eggs | ~1% | Limited flock on Deck 5 |

Rice and potatoes are not grown onboard. A hectare of rice yields about 6 tonnes per year in good conditions. Even a large onboard farm would produce only a few hundred kilograms per day, which is negligible at this scale. Staple grains are imported.

## Greenhouse Agriculture

The main onboard crops are leafy greens, tomatoes, peppers, cucumbers, strawberries, herbs, and sprouts. These are grown in nutrient film technique (NFT) and deep-water culture systems.

Realistic yields for hydroponic leafy greens are roughly 25 to 40 kg per square metre per year, or about 0.07 to 0.11 kg per square metre per day. Targeting 60 tonnes of produce per day requires about **0.6 km²** of growing area, roughly 6 percent of the ship's usable floor space.

Greenhouses are on the upper decks under translucent panels for natural light where possible, and on lower decks under LED arrays where it is not. LED lighting consumes about 4 to 6 megawatts. This is one of the largest food-related energy costs.

Growing media and nutrients are stored in bulk and recycled. Nutrient solution is filtered and reused with about 95 percent recovery.

## Aquaculture

Fish are raised in recirculating aquaculture systems (RAS): closed tanks where water is filtered by biological and mechanical processes and reused. Feed is partly insect meal and partly algae. Tilapia and catfish are preferred because they tolerate high densities and wide temperature ranges.

Fish waste is converted to nitrate and used as fertilizer for the greenhouses. This is aquaponics: fish and plants share one water system.

Ocean fishing is not practical for most of the voyage. The ship could trawl or net at anchorage, but this is restricted by the Company to reduce environmental impact and to protect the stock on which it depends.

## Nutrition

The most common deficiencies on long voyages are:

- **Vitamin C** (scurvy): prevented by fresh sprouts, citrus shipments, and a daily ration of greenhouse produce
- **Vitamin D**: UV lamps in residential areas, and vitamin D tablets for the elderly and children
- **Vitamin B12**: supplied by fortified foods and by microalgae
- **Iodine**: added to salt
- **Iron**: fortified grain and algae

The Medical Office publishes a standard ration guideline. Children receive a supplement program funded by the Company.

## Commissary and Distribution

Food is distributed through the **Commissary**, which runs about 60 kitchens and canteens across the residential terraces and the Spine. Each adult receives a meal allowance credited to their ledger account (see `economics_and_trade.md`). Children's meals are subsidized.

Meals vary by department. Engineering crews eat more protein. The Commissary tries to provide one full hot meal per shift. Meat is served about three times a week, depending on port supplies.

## Food Waste and Recycling

Food waste averages about 12 percent of the total supply, which is high by shore standards. Waste is composted in the Waste Processing Halls and fed to black soldier fly larvae, which convert it into protein for fish and poultry. Inedible residue is incinerated in the reactor-independent boilers.

## Food Safety

The Commissary operates under a Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Points (HACCP) plan. Key controls include:
- Cold chain monitoring with continuous temperature logging
- Separate preparation areas for raw proteins
- Routine testing of greenhouse water for *Salmonella* and *Listeria*
- Monthly inspection by the Medical Office

Outbreaks are reported to the Chief Medical Officer immediately. Affected food batches are destroyed, and the source is isolated.
