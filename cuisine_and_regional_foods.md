# Cuisine and Regional Foods Aboard the Halcyon Meridian

## Overview

Food supply is covered in `food_and_agriculture.md`, which explains where the ship's calories and nutrients come from. This file is about the cooking itself: what people eat, how regional food traditions survive at sea, and how the Commissary and the Aft Hall restaurants work.

The ship's cuisine is a mix of Commissary staples, imported goods bought at port, and home cooking by residents with access to small kitchens. Because the ship has residents from more than 60 countries, the range of food is wide.

## The Commissary Staples

The Commissary provides a standard set of staples, bought in bulk from port and stored in the dry holds:

- **Grains**: rice, wheat flour, oats, and corn meal
- **Pulses**: lentils, chickpeas, black beans, and dried peas
- **Oils and fats**: cooking oil, butter, and ghee
- **Dried goods**: pasta, noodles, and dried fruit
- **Fish and seafood**: tinned fish and dried fish, plus farmed tilapia and catfish (see `food_and_agriculture.md`)
- **Vegetables and herbs**: greenhouse produce, plus stored root vegetables when available

Pulses and grains are the foundation of most dishes. They provide protein and calories at low cost and keep well in storage.

## Regional Traditions

Residents bring food traditions from their home countries, and many dishes survive with local substitutions. Common examples include:

- **Rice-based dishes** from South and Southeast Asia, using Commissary rice and greenhouse herbs
- **Bean and grain stews** from Latin America and Africa, made with pulses from the holds
- **Flatbreads and wheat dishes** from the Middle East and Mediterranean, using stored flour and oil
- **Noodle and soup dishes** from East Asia, often with dried ingredients and microalgae
- **Fish preparations** from the coastal regions of many home countries, with farmed tilapia or catfish as the base

Some ingredients are hard to replace. Residents from regions with particular spices or sauces often rely on port purchases, or on small quantities brought aboard in personal stores, which are subject to the Commissary's rules on imported food.

## Fermentation

Fermentation is common on the ship. It preserves food, adds flavour, and can improve nutrition. Residents make sauerkraut, kimchi-style pickles, yoghurt, and vinegar in their kitchens. The Commissary runs a fermentation room on Deck 6, where staff produce fermented vegetables, sauces, and bread starter for the galleys.

Fermentation requires careful hygiene. The Commissary's food safety rules apply, and home fermentation is limited to approved containers (see `food_and_agriculture.md`).

## Insects and Microalgae

Insect and algae products are part of the ship's diet, and some residents cook them in traditional dishes. Black soldier fly larvae are ground into a protein powder that is used in flatbreads, sauces, and snacks. Spirulina and chlorella are added to noodles and drinks.

Some residents find these foods unfamiliar, and the Commissary labels them clearly. Insect products are not served in the Aft Hall unless customers request them.

## The Aft Hall

The Aft Hall is the main restaurant area, serving food from many regions. It has about 40 stalls, and the menus change with port calls and the Commissary's stock. Typical items include:

- Rice bowls with greenhouse vegetables and fish
- Noodle soups with microalgae or egg
- Bean stews and flatbreads
- Fresh salads with greenhouse tomatoes, cucumber, and herbs
- Desserts made with dried fruit, oats, and sugar

Prices are set by the Market Authority (see `markets_and_commerce.md`). A basic bowl costs about 8 to 12 LC, which is within reach of most workers.

## Eating Patterns

Residents usually eat at fixed times, in line with the ship's shift system (see `daily_life_and_routines.md`). Breakfast is served from 06:00, lunch from 11:00, and dinner from 17:00. Night shift workers receive meals from the Aft Hall until 01:00, and the Commissary provides packed meals for those on watch.

Families often eat together in their cabins or on their terraces. Shared meals are common on terrace common rooms, especially on festival days (see `calendar_holidays_and_festivals.md`).

## Meat and Eggs

Meat is scarce. The Commissary serves meat about three times a week, depending on port supplies (see `food_and_agriculture.md`). Poultry is raised on Deck 5, and eggs are available daily. Many residents eat more vegetarian meals than they would on shore, partly because of cost and partly because of the ship's greenhouse produce.

## Special Diets

The Commissary provides halal, kosher, vegetarian, vegan, and fasting meals on request (see `religion_and_chaplaincy.md`). Medical diets are also available, for conditions such as diabetes, coeliac disease, and kidney disease, through the hospital's dietary service (see `hospital_and_medical_care.md`).

## Food Culture on the Spine

The market halls sell fresh and prepared food from many traditions, and the Spine is full of food smells and sounds at mealtimes. Food festivals are held after major port calls, when new ingredients arrive (see `ports_of_call.md`). The biggest is the Landfall Festival (see `arts_media_and_entertainment.md`).

## Home Cooking

Most cabins have only a small kitchenette, and only Grade 0 suites have full cooking space. Many residents cook on shared terrace kitchens, which are booked through the terrace committee. Cooking gas is not used on the ship, because of fire risk, so all cooking is electric (see `ship_structure_and_layout.md`).

## Ingredients on Trial

The Commissary's food team experiments with new ingredients, such as seaweed from the aquaculture tanks and new insect products. Residents are invited to taste test and give feedback. Several recipes from these trials have become popular on the Spine.
