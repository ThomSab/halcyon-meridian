# Hospital and Medical Care Aboard the Halcyon Meridian

## Facilities

The ship's main hospital, the **Meridian Central Hospital**, occupies three decks in the forward superstructure, near the Bridge and reachable from the Spine by a dedicated elevator bank. It has:

- **1,200 hospital beds**, including 60 intensive care beds
- **8 surgical theatres**, with one dedicated to trauma and emergency surgery
- An imaging suite with CT, ultrasound, digital X-ray, and an MRI scanner
- A dialysis unit with 40 stations
- A pharmacy holding about 18 months of essential medicines
- A dental clinic
- A blood bank, with a stock of about 4,000 units in rotation

Smaller clinics operate on the residential terraces, one for every two terraces. Each clinic has a nurse, a doctor on call, and basic diagnostic equipment. Most routine complaints are handled there.

## Staff

The Medical Department has about **1,800 staff**:

| Role | Number |
|---|---|
| Physicians | 420 |
| Nurses | 640 |
| Allied health (physiotherapy, radiography, pharmacy, etc.) | 380 |
| Technicians and laboratory staff | 160 |
| Support staff (cleaning, sterile processing, porters) | 200 |

Physicians include general practitioners, anaesthetists, surgeons, psychiatrists, and paediatricians. Specialists are on rotation, with eight-month contracts for most specialist roles.

## Medical Evacuation

The ship has two helicopter pads on the aft superstructure roof. A helicopter can only reach the ship within its range from land, roughly 400 km for the aircraft the Company contracts. Across most of the Atlantic, the ship is more than 2,000 km from shore, so evacuation is not possible.

For this reason, the hospital is designed to treat most serious cases aboard. Complex cases are managed by telemedicine, with consultants at shore hospitals linked through the satellite system. The Chief Medical Officer decides whether a case is treated aboard or evacuated, and evacuations are arranged through the nearest coastal rescue service.

## Common Conditions

The most common conditions aboard are:

- **Respiratory infections**, spread in enclosed spaces. Flu season peaks in the winter months of the ship's current route.
- **Gastrointestinal illness**, including norovirus. Outbreaks are handled by the Quarantine Ward (see below).
- **Musculoskeletal injuries** from falls, including those caused by motion.
- **Vitamin D deficiency**, managed with supplements and UV lamps (see `food_and_agriculture.md`).
- **Sleep disorders**, linked to vibration and the ship's clock adjustments (see `daily_life_and_routines.md`).
- **Chronic disease**, particularly hypertension and diabetes among older residents.

## Quarantine Ward

The Quarantine Ward has 80 beds in negative-pressure isolation rooms. Air is exhausted through HEPA and UV filters, not recirculated to other parts of the ship. Staff use full protective equipment when entering. Patients are assessed daily for discharge.

During the 2069 norovirus outbreak (see `history_and_construction_timeline.md`), the ward was filled to capacity within four days. Since then, the Medical Office has trained a second quarantine team and stocked additional negative-pressure units in storage.

## Radiation Safety

Because the ship carries reactors, the hospital has a radiation protection team. The team monitors the reactor citadels and the ship's shielding, and it provides dosimeters to crew who work near the reactors. The hospital's imaging equipment is shielded and calibrated under the Chief Engineer's licence requirements.

Radiation injury is very unlikely. The Medical Office's procedures for radiation exposure are drilled twice a year.

## Mental Health Services

The Medical Department runs a mental health service with about 60 counsellors and psychiatrists. It offers individual counselling, group sessions, and crisis support. The service is busiest during long passages and after emergencies. Referrals are confidential. Residents can self-refer through the terrace clinic.

## Costs and Funding

Medical care is provided free to residents as part of their contract. The Company funds it from the operating budget and from charges to the flag registry for emergency care. Crew members receive the same care as residents.

## Records

Medical records are held electronically in the ship's health database, with a paper copy in the hospital archive. Records follow the resident to port if they leave the ship, with consent.
