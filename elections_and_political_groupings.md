# Elections and Political Groupings Aboard the Halcyon Meridian

## Overview

The ship has a working electoral system, set out in the Articles of Agreement (see `governance_and_law.md`). Elections are held for the **Residents' Council**, which has 60 seats and is elected every three years. Political life on the ship is built around wards, terraces, and the associations described in `labor_unions_and_crew_associations.md`, rather than around national parties.

## Electoral Wards

The ship is divided into 60 wards, each covering roughly 4,400 residents. Wards follow the residential towers, so that each terrace belongs to one ward. Crew blocks and the housing blocks forward of the Spine form their own wards. Ward boundaries are reviewed by an independent Boundary Commission every 10 years.

Crew are not eligible to vote for the Council unless they also live in a residential ward, and most crew do not vote in Council elections for this reason. This is one of the more debated features of the Articles.

## Eligibility

To vote, a person must:
- Be aged 16 or older
- Be registered as a resident in the ward
- Have lived aboard for at least 90 days

To stand for the Council, a candidate must be 21 or older, have lived aboard for at least one year, and be nominated by 50 residents in their ward.

## Voting

Elections are held on the intranet over a three-day period, with paper ballots available at each terrace common room for anyone who cannot vote electronically. Turnout typically runs between 55 and 70 percent. Counting is done by a neutral team from the Maritime Court, with observers from the candidates.

The electronic system is designed so that a voter's identity is separated from their vote. The Privacy Committee (see `surveillance_privacy_and_data.md`) reviews the system before each election.

## Political Groupings

There are no national parties. Candidates stand as independents, but in practice they form loose groupings. The main groupings in recent elections are:

| Grouping | Focus | Typical Support |
|---|---|---|
| **Harbour Alliance** | Stable housing, lower rents, and extended welfare | Strong among workers and Grade 2 and Grade 3 residents |
| **Terrace Forum** | Local services, terrace upkeep, and noise limits | Strong among long-term residents of the older terraces |
| **Crew Voice** | Crew pay, housing, and working conditions | Strong among skilled crew and association members |
| **Open Spine** | Market access, cultural life, and fewer restrictions | Strong among younger residents and market traders |
| **Independents** | No fixed platform | Varies widely by ward |

Groupings do not have formal membership, and they often split or change names between elections. The Harbour Alliance and the Terrace Forum have sometimes cooperated on housing issues.

## Council Business

The Council meets every fortnight in the Solarium deck office, in an open session that residents may attend. Committees cover housing, food and water, schools, health, the Spine, and the budget. Committees take evidence from the Company and from the associations, and they report to the full Council.

The Council's main powers are described in `governance_and_law.md`. In practice, the most important are:
- Ratifying the budget for resident services
- Calling referenda on food and water prices
- Referring complaints to the Maritime Court
- Electing a liaison to the Master

## Campaigning

Campaigning is allowed on the intranet and in public spaces in designated areas, such as the Spine's community halls and the market halls. Candidates may not use the Company's resources, including the Gazette, for their campaign. Campaign spending is capped at 300 LC per candidate, and campaign donations must be declared.

The Market Authority has noted that campaign posters in the market halls have affected stall rents, which the Authority has recognised as a conflict of interest. Stall holders may no longer display campaign material in their stalls.

## The Gazette and the Campaign

The Halcyon Gazette covers the elections and gives each grouping equal space in its coverage. The Gazette's independence review (see `arts_media_and_entertainment.md`) has examined this coverage, and the review found some bias toward the Harbour Alliance, which the Gazette has since corrected.

## Referenda

Referenda may be called by the Council on food and water prices, and by petition on any matter within the Council's powers. A petition needs signatures from 10 percent of residents. The most recent referendum was on the 2071 proposal to introduce microphones on the Spine, which was rejected by 58 percent (see `surveillance_privacy_and_data.md`).

## Disputes

Election disputes go to the Maritime Court. The Court has overturned two ward results since the first election: one for a counting error, and one for a breach of the campaign spending limit. Both were re-run within a month.

## Turnout and Participation

Turnout is highest in the older terraces and lowest among crew, who often work rotating shifts that make voting difficult. The Council has asked the Company to allow voting from shift stations and to extend the voting window for crew. The Company's response is due in the next budget round.
