# Piracy, Security, and Threats Aboard the Halcyon Meridian

## Threat Overview

A vessel of this size is an attractive target for criminal groups, but it is also hard to attack. The main threats are:

- **Piracy and armed boarding**, mainly in ports and at anchorage near high-risk coasts
- **Stowaways**, who may hide in freight containers during port calls
- **Cyber attacks**, against navigation, power control, and the ledger system
- **GNSS interference**, which can confuse satellite positioning in some regions
- **Drone and small-boat surveillance**, which may precede other attacks
- **Insider threats**, including sabotage and ledger fraud (see `crime_and_justice_in_practice.md`)

The ship's risk varies greatly by location. Mid-ocean is low risk. Anchorage and port approaches are higher risk, particularly off West Africa.

## Route Planning

The Company avoids high-risk waters where possible. The standard route avoids the Gulf of Guinea and the coasts of West Africa (see `navigation_and_weather.md`). Freight contracts that require calls in higher-risk areas are reviewed by the Board, and the Master must approve any such call.

The Company works with a maritime security consultancy, which provides threat assessments before each port call. Assessments include recent incident reports, local conditions, and advice on anchorage positions.

## Security Team

The ship has a **Maritime Security Detachment** of 120 contracted officers. Their role is to protect the ship against boarding and to support the Wardens in incidents. They are armed only where the flag state permits and the port state allows it. Armed guards are not carried in ports that prohibit firearms, and the Company respects those rules.

The Detachment is trained in:
- Anti-boarding procedures and the use of water cannon
- Securing the Bridge and the reactor citadels
- Search and inspection of freight containers
- Response to stowaways, mass arrival, and hostage situations

## Physical Defences

The ship's defences include:

- **Secure rooms** on the Spine and in each terrace block, which can be locked from inside
- **Water cannon** mounted on the superstructure and on the forward and aft decks, to deter small boats
- **Razor wire and barriers** on the lower decks and the boarding points
- **Restricted access** to the Bridge, the reactor citadels, and the City Operations Centre, controlled by biometric locks
- **Perimeter lighting** and camera coverage on the hull

The ship's height and freeboard make boarding from small boats difficult. Most attacks would need to be made from a larger vessel or with a ladder from a moving boat, which is hard in a swell.

## Stowaways

Stowaways are a common problem for ships in port, and the Company has had several incidents. The freight yard is sealed during port calls, and containers are scanned by X-ray and by carbon dioxide sensors that detect human breathing. The Security Detachment checks each container before it is loaded into the holds.

When a stowaway is found, the Wardens hold them in a separate room, the Medical Office assesses their condition, and the case is referred to the port authorities. Stowaways are not treated as residents, and the Company's duty to them is limited to humane treatment and medical care.

## Cyber Security

The ship's navigation, power control, and ledger systems are separated by firewalls and monitored by a Cyber Security Team of about 40 staff. Critical systems do not connect directly to the satellite network. Software updates are tested offline before installation.

A cyber attack on the ledger in 2071 froze resident accounts for 18 hours. The Company has since added offline copies of the ledger and manual procedures for paying wages and rent.

## GNSS Interference

Satellite navigation can be jammed or spoofed in some regions. The ship uses inertial navigation and radar to cross-check its position, and the Navigation Officer is trained to detect and report interference. If interference is detected, the ship alerts other vessels and the regional coastguard.

## Anchorage Security

At anchorage, the ship maintains a watch with radar and cameras, and small boats are challenged at a set distance. The Security Detachment keeps a boat on the water during port calls, with the authority to warn off and, where permitted, use water cannon. Anchorage is avoided if the threat level is rated high.

## Incidents

The Company's security record includes several incidents:

- **2069**, at an anchorage off West Africa, a group of skiffs approached the ship at night. The Security Detachment used water cannon and warning lights, and the boats turned away. No one was hurt.
- **2071**, a ledger attack (described above), with no physical threat.
- **2073**, a stowaway found in a freight container during a call at a northern European port. The stowaway was handed to port authorities.

## Drills

Security drills are held monthly. Each drill tests a different scenario, such as a boarding at anchorage, a bomb threat, or a fire set by an intruder. Results are reviewed by the Chief Warden and the Master, and the procedures are updated after each drill.
