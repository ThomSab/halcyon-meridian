# Religion and Chaplaincy Aboard the Halcyon Meridian

## Overview

Residents of the *Halcyon Meridian* come from more than 60 countries and hold a wide range of beliefs. The largest religious groups are Christian, Muslim, Hindu, and Buddhist. A large share of residents describe themselves as non-religious, and no single faith forms a majority. The Articles of Agreement guarantee freedom of religion and freedom from religion.

The **Chaplaincy** is a Company-funded department that serves all faiths and none. It has about 60 staff, including chaplains from several traditions, and it runs the ship's religious facilities.

## Places of Worship

The ship has **multi-faith chapels** on decks 11, 16, and 21, each with a central space and side rooms for smaller groups. There are also dedicated rooms for specific traditions:

- Two mosques, one forward and one aft, with washing facilities and space for Friday prayers
- A Hindu shrine room on Deck 16
- A Buddhist meditation room on Deck 21
- A Jewish prayer room on Deck 11, with a kosher kitchen in the Commissary
- A Christian chapel in each of the three multi-faith spaces
- A quiet room on each terrace for personal reflection

Facilities are designed to be used by any resident. The Chaplaincy schedules shared use of spaces so that conflicts do not arise.

## Prayer Times and Qibla

Prayer times are difficult on a moving ship. Ship time is fixed, but the sun's position changes with the ship's longitude, so the times for dawn, midday, and sunset are different from those on shore. The Chaplaincy publishes a **daily prayer timetable** for each tradition, calculated from the ship's GPS position and updated at midnight.

The direction of prayer for Muslims (qibla) also changes as the ship moves. The mosques display a qibla indicator, calculated from the ship's current position, and the Chaplaincy publishes the bearing in the daily timetable.

## Fasting

During Ramadan, the fast runs from dawn to sunset. At sea, the times are set from the ship's position and published by the Chaplaincy. Because the ship's clock is fixed to the departure port (see `daily_life_and_routines.md`), the timing of the fast can drift relative to shore. The Chaplaincy works with the Commissary to adjust meal times during Ramadan so that residents can eat before dawn and after sunset.

## Dietary Rules

The Commissary provides:
- Halal and kosher meals on request, prepared in separate kitchens or equipment
- Vegetarian and vegan options at every service
- Fasting meal options during religious fasts

The Commissary's procedures are checked by the Chaplaincy and the Medical Office (see `food_and_agriculture.md`).

## Chaplains and Counselling

Chaplains provide pastoral care to all residents. Services include:
- Bereavement support, including funeral arrangements and memorial services
- Counselling for people facing family crises, long-term homesickness, or loss
- Visits to the Quarantine Ward and the hospital, on request
- Support for end-of-life care, in cooperation with the Medical Office (see `elder_care_and_end_of_life.md`)

Chaplains are bound by the same confidentiality rules as medical staff. They may refer residents to the mental health service (see `hospital_and_medical_care.md`).

## Marriage and Funerals

Religious marriages are performed by chaplains of the couple's tradition, and the Master may also witness civil marriages (see `governance_and_law.md`). Funerals are held in the chapels or in the Spine's community halls, depending on the faith and the number of mourners.

Burials at sea follow the procedures of each tradition, within the limits of environmental rules (see `environmental_impact_and_ocean_ecology.md`). Many residents choose to have their remains returned to port for burial.

## Interfaith Work

The Chaplaincy runs regular interfaith events, including an annual service held on the Solarium deck and a shared meal at major festivals (see `calendar_holidays_and_festivals.md`). Disputes between groups are referred to the Chaplaincy's conciliation team, which reports to the Master.

## Staffing

The Chaplaincy has about 60 staff. Most are volunteers from the resident population, who receive training from the Chaplaincy and are paid a small stipend. Chaplains are required to have completed the Chaplaincy's induction course, which covers the ship's legal framework, the Articles, and mental health referral pathways.
